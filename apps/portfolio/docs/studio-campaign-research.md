# Studio Campaign Research: $500 Local SMB Lead Gen (Glendale / Pasadena / Burbank / SFV)

Researched 2026-11-10. Benchmarks come mostly from vendor and agency blogs, not audited platform data, so treat every figure as directional. Where no source was found, the text says so. Items marked **(rec)** are my recommendations, not sourced facts.

**Bottom line:** $500 cannot buy statistical certainty or exit Meta's learning phase. Use it to test two or three hooks and get 10-25 email signups. Free and relationship channels (Google Business Profile, chambers, referral partners, a printed audit) should carry most of the real pipeline.

## 1. Channel comparison

| Channel | Cost evidence (as of date) | Min viable budget | Fit for $500 |

Sources: [WordStream FB benchmarks](https://www.wordstream.com/blog/facebook-ads-benchmarks-2025), [Metadata B2B benchmarks](https://metadata.io/b2b-advertising-benchmarks), [WordStream Google benchmarks](https://wordstream.com/blog/2025-google-ads-benchmarks), [Business services CPC](https://totalproductmarketing.com/marketing-insights/cpc-google-search-ads-business-services/), [LSA overview](https://ppc.land/local-services-ads/), [Nextdoor cost](https://thestacc.com/blog/nextdoor-for-business/), [Nextdoor self-serve](https://browsermedia.agency/blog/nextdoor-self-serve-ads/), [LinkedIn cost](https://benly.ai/learn/linkedin-ads/linkedin-ads-cost), [Yelp cost](https://www.webfx.com/blog/ppc/yelp-advertising-costs/).

**Meta learning-phase math.** Meta's common guidance is ~50 optimization events per ad set within 7 days ([AdManage](https://admanage.ai/blog/facebook-ads-learning-phase-guide)). Budget needed is CPA x 50 / 7 per day: at a $40 CPL that is ~$285/day, versus the ~$8/day we can afford. We will sit in "Learning Limited" no matter what. Mitigations: one campaign, one or two ad sets (not five), optimize for landing-page views or link clicks (a higher-volume event), and don't edit ads mid-flight ([GoMarble](https://www.gomarble.ai/blog/meta-ads-learning-phase-understanding)). Some practitioners say the 50-event rule is dated at high CPAs ([RocketShip](https://www.rocketshiphq.com/?p=18931)), so don't over-weight it.

## 2. Budget split and 4-week flight **(rec)**

| Bucket | Amount | Daily | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| Google Search (exact/phrase, 2 ad groups) | $200 | ~$7 | High-intent: "restaurant online ordering," "small business IT support Glendale" |
| Meta (1 campaign, 2 ad sets) | $225 | ~$8 | Hooks and creative test, incl. bilingual and security-led |
| Nextdoor | $50 | ~$2 | Cheap probe, weeks 3-4 only if Meta CTR is healthy |
| Reserve | $25 | n/a | Retargeting boost on best-performing creative / printing the audit |

**Schedule**
- Week 1: launch Google plus 2 Meta ads (one security-led, one revenue-led). Do not touch anything for 5 days.
- Week 2: cut the Meta ad with the lower CTR; add bilingual variant.
- Week 3: put weeks 1-2 winners at ~70% of remaining spend; Nextdoor probe.
- Week 4: run the winner only; write up the result. Cap total spend at $500 including reserve.

**Expected volume at planning numbers:** ~35 Google clicks at 10-20% opt-in = 4-7 signups; ~150-250 Meta clicks (at ~$1-1.50 CPC, rec assumption, check in Ads Manager) at 8-15% = 12-35 signups. Realistic total: 10-30 signups, 1-3 booked calls.

**Thresholds**

| Metric | Kill | Hold | Scale |
|---|---|---|---|
| Meta CTR (link) | <0.6% after 1,000 impressions | 0.6-1.0% | >1.0% |
| Landing-page opt-in rate | <5% after 50 visits | 5-10% | >10% (lead-magnet pages are cited at 5-15%; vendor range) |
| Cost per email signup | >$40 after $75 spent | $20-40 | <$20 |
| Signup to booked call | 0 calls after 15 signups: fix the nurture, not the ad | n/a | >10% |

Rationale: a single $3,500 ordering build covers the whole ad budget seven times over, so even $100-150 per booked call is acceptable at 20-30% close. The binding constraint is sample size, not CPL.

Landing-page benchmarks: [Focus Digital](https://focus-digital.co/lead-magnet-conversion-rate-by-industry/), [Leadpages](https://www.leadpages.com/blog/landing-page-conversion-benchmarks-2026).

## 5. Landing page and tracking

**Conversion**
- Ask for **email only** (optionally first name). An analysis of 184 form tests found each extra field cuts conversion ~6.4% on average, with 3-4 fields optimal ([Neil Patel summary](https://neilpatel.com/marketing-stats/conversion-rate-by-form-fields/)); lead-magnet opt-in benchmarks range 5-20% (vendor blogs).
- CTA copy: "Send me the free checklist" beats "Submit." Subtext: "1 page, 12 checks, 10 minutes."
- Social proof with no clients yet **(rec)**: your engineering background, a self-audit of your own site with real scores (Lighthouse, WCAG), a sample checklist page preview, a named local credential or chamber membership once you have it. Do not fabricate testimonials.
- Mobile first (most local ad clicks are phone), single column, form above the fold, page weight small (Lighthouse >90).
- One landing page variant per campaign (headline matches the ad).

**Tracking**
- UTM convention **(rec)**: `utm_source={google|meta|nextdoor|print|chamber}`, `utm_medium={cpc|paid_social|qr|referral}`, `utm_campaign={c1_delivery_fees|c2_pos_wifi|c3_bilingual|c4_accessibility}`, `utm_content={variant}`. Lowercase, underscores, no spaces. Print QR codes carry UTMs too.
- Meta: install Pixel and fire a standard `Lead` event on signup; for the Conversions API, generate one `event_id` and send the same value from browser and server, and confirm "Deduplicated" in Events Manager ([Meta docs](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/marketing-api/conversions-api/deduplicate-pixel-and-server-events), [Stape guide](https://stape.io/blog/how-to-set-up-facebook-event-deduplication-in-google-tag-manager)). At this volume, Pixel alone is enough; add CAPI only if cheap.
- Google Ads: conversion tag on the thank-you state, count one per signup; import as primary conversion.
- Keep a simple sheet of source, signup date, call booked, deal.

**Privacy and email law**
- CCPA applies if you hit one of three thresholds: revenue over $26.625M (CPPA 2025 adjustment), 100,000+ California consumers, or 50%+ of revenue from selling/sharing data ([CPPA](https://www.cppa.ca.gov/regulations/cpi_adjustment.html)). A new studio likely is not covered, but still publish a plain privacy policy naming the tools (pixel, email provider) and promise no selling of emails. I did not find a source on notice-at-collection details (see Cal. Code Regs. tit. 11 § 7012).
- CAN-SPAM: honor opt-outs within 10 business days, include a valid physical postal address, a clear unsubscribe, accurate sender and subject lines ([Transcend](https://transcend.io/blog/can-spam-act)); verify against the FTC guide, which I did not retrieve. Penalties are per email (~$51,744-53,088 per sources). Use a PO box or virtual mailbox, not your home address. Mailbox providers expect faster unsubscribes than the legal 10 days.
- Add an unchecked-by-default newsletter consent line on the form: "Email me the checklist and occasional tips. Unsubscribe anytime." (rec).

## 6. Four campaigns ready to launch

Character limits are the platforms' standard limits as I know them: Google RSA headlines 30 chars, descriptions 90; Meta headline ~40 and primary text ~125 before truncation. Verify in the ad UI.

### C1: "Keep Your Delivery Margin" (revenue-led, restaurants)
- **Audience:** Meta, 10-12 mile radius of Glendale 91203 and Pasadena 91101, owners/managers and restaurant interests (e.g., restaurant management, small business owners); Google Search exact/phrase: `[restaurant online ordering system]`, `"commission free online ordering"`, `"doordash alternative restaurant"`.
- **Offer:** Free checklist plus free 20-min "delivery fee math" call.
- **Copy (Meta):**
  1. H: "DoorDash takes up to 30%." P: "On $10k of delivery that is $3,000 a month. Get the free checklist to see what direct ordering would keep."
  2. H: "Own your online orders" P: "One-time $3,500 build, no per-order commission. Download the 1-page checklist."
  3. H: "Your customers, your data" P: "Delivery apps own the customer list. See how to take it back. Free checklist."
- **Google:** H1 "Online Ordering, No Commission" / H2 "Glendale Restaurant Websites" / D "Fixed-price direct ordering. Free checklist + 20-min call."
- **Creative:** Static image: a receipt with "$3,000" circled, then "$0" next to a phone with a direct-order screen. Real numbers only.
- **LP/UTM:** `/checklist?v=delivery` ; `utm_campaign=c1_delivery_fees`.
- **Budget:** $200 (Google $120, Meta $80). **Success:** Google signup cost <$40, 1+ booked call.

### C2: "Is Your Guest Wi-Fi on Your POS Network?" (security-led)
- **Audience:** Meta, 10-mile radius Burbank 91502 / SFV (Studio City, Sherman Oaks), interests: small business owner, retail, restaurant management; Google: `"separate guest wifi pos"`, `"small business wifi security"`, `"pci wifi requirements"` (phrase), plus negatives (jobs, free, login, router password).
- **Offer:** Free checklist with the 5-minute "shop network self-check."
- **Copy:**
  1. H: "Guest Wi-Fi next to your POS?" P: "If customers can join the same network as your register, that is a risk. A 5-minute check, free."
  2. H: "5 minutes. 1 page. Zero jargon." P: "Check your shop's Wi-Fi, backups and MFA before something goes wrong. Free checklist."
  3. H: "Is your router still 'admin'?" P: "Most shop networks were set up once and forgotten. Run the free checklist."
- **Creative:** Simple two-lane diagram, "Guests" and "POS," with a red line crossing between them in the "before" version.
- **LP/UTM:** `?v=pos_wifi`; `utm_campaign=c2_pos_wifi`. Tone: no fear-mongering, no invented statistics.
- **Budget:** $125. **Success:** CTR >1%, signup rate >8%.

### C3: Bilingual "Local Neighbor" (Armenian / Korean)
- **Audience:** Meta, 8-mile radius Glendale 91205/91203 (Armenian); Koreatown/Glendale/Burbank-adjacent (Korean) as separate ad sets so copy matches. Use language targeting (Armenian, Korean) plus owner/small-business interests. Native-speaker review of all copy before launch (do not machine-translate-and-publish).
- **Offer:** Same checklist, offered in a bilingual landing page; call offered in language if you have a partner or interpreter. If you can't serve in-language, say so honestly and offer English calls.
- **Copy (English base; translate and review):**
  1. H: "Free website + Wi-Fi checklist" P: "Built for Glendale shops and restaurants. Plain language, one page."
  2. H: "Your neighbor in tech" P: "A local software engineer helping small businesses with websites, Wi-Fi and security. Free checklist, English and [Armenian/Korean]."
  3. H: "Stop paying 30% to apps" P: "Direct ordering for restaurants. Free checklist."
- **Creative:** Photo of a real local storefront (with permission) plus a short line in the target language.
- **LP/UTM:** `/checklist/hy` and `/checklist/ko`; `utm_campaign=c3_bilingual`, `utm_content=hy|ko`.
- **Budget:** $100 (50/50). **Success:** signup cost within 1.5x of C1/C2; track qualitatively in calls. Small audiences exhaust quickly: keep frequency under ~3.
- Also use the free channels here first: Armenian American Chamber, Korean groups, and in-person introductions will likely beat ads.

### C4: "Accessibility Is Not an Overlay" (education-led, professional offices and retail)
- **Audience:** Google Search: `"website accessibility audit small business"`, `"ada website compliance california"` (phrase, with negatives for "lawsuit help" if lawyer-like intent is unwanted); Meta retargeting of C1-C3 site visitors.
- **Offer:** Free 12-point accessibility and security checklist section; free 20-min call.
- **Copy:**
  1. H: "ADA site suits hit small shops" P: "California is where most land. A real fix is accessible code, not a widget. Free checklist."
  2. H: "A widget will not save you" P: "Overlays have not stopped lawsuits. See what to check instead. Free."
  3. H: "Is your site accessible?" P: "A 10-minute self-check for owners. No jargon."
- **Creative:** Screenshot-style before/after of a low-contrast button fixed.
- **LP/UTM:** `?v=access`; `utm_campaign=c4_accessibility`. Educational tone; do not imply legal advice.
- **Budget:** $50 plus reserve. **Success:** CTR >1% on retargeting; any call booked.

## 7. Six-email nurture sequence (after checklist download)

| # | Timing | Subject line | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Immediate | "Your checklist (plus the one thing to do first)" | Deliver the PDF, highlight the single quickest win, set expectations (1 email/week). |
| 2 | Day 2 | "The 5-minute Wi-Fi check for your shop" | Quick win on guest/POS network separation; builds authority. |
| 3 | Day 5 | "What delivery apps really cost you" | Fee math, soft intro to direct ordering ($3,500 fixed price, no commission). |
| 4 | Day 9 | "Why a widget will not protect your website" | Accessibility/Unruh education without fear; explain what a real fix looks like. |
| 5 | Day 14 | "A shop like yours: before and after" | One short case (a self-audit or a pilot client with permission); never fabricate. |
| 6 | Day 19 | "Want me to look at yours? 20 minutes, free" | Direct CTA to book the call; reply-to goes to a human. |

Each email: physical address and one-click unsubscribe in the footer; plain text or light HTML; UTM on every link (`utm_medium=email`, `utm_campaign=nurture_n`).
