## FDA/EPA fish advice — live, and still scoped to ages 1–11

Title, verbatim: *"Advice about Eating Fish — For Those Who Might Become or Are
Pregnant or Breastfeeding and **Children Ages 1 - 11 Years**."* Revised Oct 2021,
content current as of 2024-03-05, live and unretired.
<https://www.fda.gov/food/consumers/advice-about-eating-fish>

Servings: "1 ounce at age 1 to 3 / 2 ounces at age 4 to 7 / 3 ounces at age 8 to
10 / 4 ounces at age 11." **"Eat 2 servings a week from the 'Best Choices' list."**

⚠️ **Batch-1's finding re-confirmed: no serving amount exists below 12 months.**
The smallest issued figure is 1 oz at age 1.

But FDA *does* issue an age — Q&A III.2: "you can introduce nutritious foods like
fish… once they are **about 6 months of age**." Strongest food-specific age
citation in this batch.

### The "even lower in mercury" sub-list — a second, finer tier

> "children should only be fed fish from the 'Best Choices' list that are even
> lower in mercury – these fish are anchovies, **Atlantic mackerel**, catfish,
> clams, crab, crawfish, flounder, haddock, mullet, oysters, plaice, **pollock**,
> salmon, sardines, scallops, shad, shrimp, sole, squid, tilapia, **trout**, and
> whiting."

🚩 **Cod, Pacific chub mackerel and canned light tuna are NOT on this sub-list**
despite being Best Choices. Don't flatten the distinction.

## 🚨 The mackerel trap — worse than "King vs Atlantic"

Source: **FDA Seafood List** (updated 2026-01-29), whose column header states
*"Vernacular names are prohibited from being used."*

| Species | FDA market name | FDA common name | Prohibited vernaculars | Tier |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| *S. scombrus* | Mackerel | **Atlantic Mackerel** | Common, Boston, Caballa | **Best + sub-list** |
| *S. japonicus* | Mackerel, Chub | **Pacific Chub Mackerel** | Common, Japanese, Pacific, **Spanish** | Best (not sub-list) |
| *Scomberomorus maculatus* | Mackerel, Spanish | Atlantic Spanish Mackerel | Bay, Spotted | Good |
| *Scomberomorus cavalla* | **Mackerel, King or Spanish** | **King Mackerel** | — | **AVOID** |
| *Scomberomorus regalis* | Mackerel, Spanish | Cero | Pintado, Sierra, **King Mackerel** | Good |

**Three citable collisions:**
1. FDA's own *acceptable market name* for King mackerel (avoid tier) contains the
   word **"Spanish"** — a package reading "Spanish Mackerel" may lawfully be
   *S. cavalla*.
2. "Spanish Mackerel" is simultaneously a *prohibited vernacular* for
   *S. japonicus* (Best Choice).
3. "Common Mackerel" maps to both *S. scombrus* and *S. japonicus*.

**These must never be one canon entry.** Name the species on the page.

🚩 **Unsettled — needs a human decision.** Korean 고등어 is *Scomber japonicus*,
which FDA calls **Pacific chub mackerel**, not *S. scombrus* "Atlantic mackerel".
**The roster currently names the food "Atlantic mackerel (고등어)", which points
the name, the citation and the `fdcId` at different species.** Both are Best
Choices so the safety verdict is identical, but only Atlantic mackerel is on the
"even lower" sub-list and USDA record 175120 is *Atlantic*. Pick one and align
all three.

## 🚨 The tuna trap and "ahi"

FDA Q&A I.3: canned light = Best Choices; **albacore/white and yellowfin = Good
Choices; bigeye = Choices to Avoid.** Q&A VI.1: "canned light tuna can be a mix
of a variety of generally smaller tuna species, most often skipjack" — so
"light" is a *mercury-tier* term, not a species term. Q&A VI.3: albacore
"typically contains **three times more mercury** than canned light tuna… have
only 1 serving and **no other fish that week**."

**"Ahi", sourced primarily.** FDA Seafood List lists "Ahi" as a prohibited
vernacular for ***both*** *T. obesus* (**bigeye — Avoid**) and *T. albacares*
(**yellowfin — Good**), and both lawfully bear the single market name "Tuna".
**Menu or counter "ahi" carries no tier information at all.** A naming fact, not
an inference.

## Cooking temperatures (USDA FSIS, updated 2025-04-14)

Beef/pork/veal/**lamb** steaks, chops, roasts **145 °F + 3 min rest** · ground
meats **160 °F** · **all poultry incl. legs, thighs and giblets 165 °F** · eggs
160 °F · **fish & shellfish 145 °F** · leftovers 165 °F.

✅ **This resolves batch-1's unverified item: fish 145 °F is real and current.**

**Duck** — FSIS: pieces including "the breast, drumsticks, thighs, and wings
should be cooked until they reach… **165 °F**", and duck at 165 °F "may still be
pink in the center" — colour is not a doneness test, which matters because duck
is culturally served rare.

🚩 **Beef liver — FSIS issues NO temperature.** Not on the chart (the beef row
covers steaks/chops/roasts), not in *Beef from Farm to Table*. **Publish no
number.** Chicken liver *is* covered, as a giblet → 165 °F.

## Bones — what is actually issued

CDC: remove all fat, skin and bones before cooking (⚠️ carried from batch 1, not
re-verified). CPS, live: "Don't feed your baby… **fish with bones**." NHS, live:
"remove hard pips and stones from fruits, and **bones from meat or fish**."

🚩 **No body uses the term "pin bone."** Cod, mackerel, trout and pollock all
carry them, but the tweezers technique is `common_practice`. The guideline
content is: bones out, and out **before** cooking.

## 🚩 Liver and vitamin A — the honest position

**The issued number.** NIH ODS: Tolerable Upper Intake Level for **preformed**
vitamin A, **birth to 12 months = 600 µg/day**. Beef liver, pan fried, 3 oz =
**6,582 µg RAE (731% DV)**. Chronic intake above the UL "can cause congenital
birth defects."
<https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminA-HealthProfessional/>

**Composition, USDA-verified per 100 g:** beef liver braised **9,440 µg RAE**
(168626) · pan-fried 7,740 (168627) · chicken liver simmered 3,980 (171061) ·
pan-fried 4,300 (174491).

**Derived arithmetic — NOT issued by anyone.** Dividing the UL by the density:
600 µg is reached at roughly **6 g** braised beef liver, **8 g** pan-fried, or
**15 g** simmered chicken liver. A 10 g taste of pan-fried beef liver is ~774 µg,
about **1.3× the infant daily UL**. Publish the two source numbers; badge any
gram figure as arithmetic.

**🚩 No body issues an infant frequency cap.** Confirmed absent from AAP, CDC,
FDA, CPS, WHO and every NHS baby page. NHS's "**Do not eat liver… more than once
a week**" is on a general-population vitamin A page that **contains nothing about
children or babies**. EFSA's 2024 UL re-evaluation is paywalled (HTTP 402) — quote
no EFSA infant figure.

➡️ **"About once a week" for an infant is `common_practice`** — an extrapolation
from a UK adult rule onto a body weight it was never written for. The only
infant-applicable issued figure is the 600 µg/day UL. Say exactly that.

## Hard prohibitions

FDA Q&A VIII.2: children "should only eat foods with fish, meat, poultry, or eggs
that have been cooked to safe internal temperatures… **This includes not eating
raw fish**, like that found in some sushi or sashimi." → no 회, no sashimi, no
rare duck breast, no pink lamb centre.

FDA Q&A V.4: "**Mercury is found throughout the tissue in fish, so cleaning or
cooking will not reduce the amount of mercury**" — kills any "cooking makes it
safer" framing.

## Per food — all eleven are heme; no vitamin-C pairing required

### Lamb (shoulder or leg)
Age ~6m (general). Choking **modify** — "chunks of meat" is an AAP avoid-until-4
entry. Not a US-9 allergen. **Fe 2.12, Zn 5.01** shoulder broiled `fdcId`
**174382**; shoulder braised Fe 2.39, Zn 6.08 **172501**; leg roasted Fe 1.98,
Zn 4.66 **172485**. Shoulder edges leg on both; **the zinc figures (4.5–6.1 mg)
are the highest non-liver values in the batch.** FSIS 145 °F + 3 min; 160 °F
ground.

### Pork shoulder
Age ~6m. Choking **modify**. Not a US-9 allergen. **HEME, weakest red meat here:
Fe 1.32, Zn 3.71** roasted `fdcId` **167844**; lean only Fe 1.50 **167846**.
FSIS 145 °F + 3 min. 🚩 **Fresh pork only** — bacon, ham, pancetta, chorizo and
sausage are excluded twice over: sodium (<24m) and AAP's "meat sticks or
sausages" choking entry.

### Turkey dark meat
Age ~6m. AAP's "well-cooked, finely chopped chicken" is the nearest issued
poultry phrasing; extending it to turkey is `common_practice`. **Fe 1.43, Zn
3.51** roasted `fdcId` **171091** — ~27% more iron and ~83% more zinc than
chicken thigh (Fe 1.13, Zn 1.92, **172388**, re-verified today). **Dark meat
only**; do not carry these to breast. 🚩 **Avoid brine-injected "with added
solution" birds** (174508, 174510) — ~25% *lower* iron plus added sodium. FSIS
165 °F.

### Beef liver
Age ~6m for iron-rich animal foods generally. **No body names liver as an infant
food, and none prohibits it** — state that absence honestly. **HEME, densest
iron+zinc available: Fe 6.54, Zn 5.30** braised `fdcId` **168626**; pan-fried Fe
6.17 **168627**. 🚨 **Vitamin A is the governing constraint, not iron** — see
above. 🚩 No FSIS temperature exists. 🚩 No issued infant frequency cap.
**This is the only food in the canon needing a frequency limit — be exact that
the limit is convention and the UL is the guideline.**

### Chicken liver
**HEME — the single highest iron in the entire canon: Fe 11.6** simmered `fdcId`
**171061**; **Fe 12.9** pan-fried **174491**. About 2× beef liver and 5× beef
mince. Vitamin A 3,980–4,300 µg RAE/100 g — roughly half beef liver's density but
still ~6.6× the infant daily UL per 100 g. ✅ **FSIS 165 °F** (giblets row) —
unlike beef liver, this temperature *is* issued. 🚩 Pâté and parfait excluded
separately on salt and, commercially, mould-ripening.

### Cod
Age ~6m (**FDA's own statement**). **Best Choice — but NOT on the "even lower"
sub-list.** Choking **modify — bones**. **US-9 `fish`, and cod is one of FDA's
three statutory examples** ("bass, flounder, cod") — strongest allergen citation
of the five fish. **Fe 0.49, Zn 0.58, Na 78** `fdcId` **171956** — lowest iron of
the five; this entry earns its place on allergen introduction and flake texture,
not iron. Say so. FSIS 145 °F. No salt cod.

### Atlantic mackerel (고등어)
🚨 **Highest-flag naming item in the batch — see the mackerel table. Never merge
with King mackerel; name the species on the page.** Choking **modify — heavily
pin-boned**. US-9 `fish`. **Fe 1.57, Zn 0.94, Na 83 — highest iron of the five
fish** `fdcId` **175120** (*S. scombrus*). ⚠️ **No verified USDA record for cooked
*S. japonicus* was located** — if the entry becomes chub mackerel, the `fdcId` is
unverified. FSIS 145 °F. No 고등어회, no salt-cured 자반고등어.

### Rainbow trout
**Best Choice AND on the "even lower" sub-list.** 🚩 **But FDA singles trout out
for *caught* fish**: "larger carp, catfish, **trout** and perch are more likely to
have fish advisories", and where no advisory exists "**children under the age of
six should limit their consumption of these fish to 1 to 2 ounces per week**".
That is the only age-banded ounce figure in the advice whose wording reaches below
age 1 — **but it is written for caught fish, not farmed retail trout. Do not
generalise it.** Choking **modify — the most pin-boned presentation in the batch**.
**Fe 0.36, Zn 0.54** farmed `fdcId` **173718**; wild Fe 0.38 **175155** — a 0.02 mg
difference, not worth editorialising.

### Canned light skipjack tuna
**Best Choice; not on the sub-list.** 🚨 Tier depends entirely on which tuna — see
the tuna trap. Choking **low-to-modify**: flaked and boneless, so no bone rule;
its risk is dryness at the back of the mouth, which is `common_practice`. Moisten.
US-9 `fish`. **Fe 1.67, Zn 0.66, Na 219** canned in water drained `fdcId`
**334194**; **no-salt-added Fe 1.53, Na 50** `fdcId` **171986**.
🚩 **A 4.4× sodium gap, USDA-verified (219 → 50 mg/100 g).** Under the <24m sodium
rule this is the most actionable purchasing instruction in the batch — draining
does not remove it, buy no-salt-added. No cooking temperature (retort-sterilised).
🚩 Never substitute albacore into this entry.

### Pollock (명태)
**Best Choice AND on the "even lower" sub-list** — one of the safest entries in
the canon. Choking **modify — bones**. US-9 `fish`. **Fe 0.29, Zn 0.44, Na 166**
Alaska `fdcId` **173726**; Atlantic Fe 0.59 **174237**; **additive-treated Na
419** `fdcId` **173681**. 🚩 **Sodium trap: 166 → 419 mg/100 g before any
seasoning** — buy untreated fillet. 🚩 **The Korean preparations are the real
hazard and are all excluded on sodium**: 북어/황태 (dried), 코다리 (semi-dried),
자반 (salted), 명란 (salt-cured roe). ⚠️ No USDA record or sodium figure verified
for any — frame qualitatively, publish no number. Surimi is a separate product.

### Duck leg
Age ~6m. Choking **modify**. Not a US-9 allergen. **HEME — roughly double chicken
thigh: Fe 2.33, Na 108** braised skinless `fdcId` **171113**; **Fe 2.08** roasted
with skin **171112**; vs chicken thigh Fe 1.13 (**172388**) → **~1.8–2.1×**.
⚠️ **Zinc for both duck-leg records is absent from USDA SR Legacy — unverified.
Do not borrow 2.60 mg from the all-parts record (172411).** FSIS 165 °F, and
colour is not a doneness test. 🚩 **Confit excluded** (salt-cured); Peking and
Chinese roast duck excluded (added sugar and sodium) — no figures verified.

## Disagreements to surface, not resolve

- **Oily fish frequency.** NHS caps children's oily fish **by sex** — "boys… no more than 4 portions a week, girls no more than 2" — on **dioxins/PCBs**, and names mackerel and trout as oily fish. FDA/EPA issues no sex-specific child cap and tiers on **methylmercury** alone. NHS also says canned tuna doesn't count as oily fish; FDA doesn't address it. Neither reaches below 12 months.
- **Shark/swordfish/marlin age.** NHS: all children under 16 should avoid. FDA/EPA: "Choices to Avoid" with no age ceiling. Same direction, different construction.
- **Liver.** NHS caps at once a week for the general population and says nothing about children; NIH ODS sets a 600 µg/day infant UL and says nothing about frequency. **Neither is an infant liver rule. Report the gap; do not manufacture a bridge.**
- 🚩 **Allergen re-exposure spacing — a human must choose the wording.** FDA advises watching "for **several days** before feeding your child fish a second time" while the same paragraph explicitly rejects delayed introduction. RULES bans the superficially identical "wait 3–5 days between new foods". FDA's sentence is about spacing first and second exposure to *the same* allergen, not spacing *different* foods — but the two read almost identically to a parent.
