## Background

**No AAP or CDC infant food list names cucumber.** NHS covers it, but only
inside its general preparation guidance, in a list of vegetables to cut into
narrow batons. So everything in this record is a category rule applied to
cucumber, not a food-specific ruling anyone issued. That is worth saying out
loud, because the category rule it lands in is strict and the food looks
harmless.

Two category rules apply. AAP's is about geometry: "do not feed children younger
than 4 round, firm food unless it is chopped completely." A raw cucumber round
is exactly that shape — a firm disc, roughly airway-sized, with a smooth rim.
CDC's is about hard raw vegetables generally, the same line that puts raw
carrots on the list.

**The skin is the specific hazard**, and it is what makes cucumber different
from a soft raw fruit. Cucumber flesh is watery and yields; the skin is tough,
does not break down under gums, and holds a bitten piece together as an intact
ring rather than letting it collapse. Peeling removes that.

NHS gives the fixes directly: cut vegetables like carrots, peppers, cucumber and
celery into **narrow batons**; remove the skin, which "makes it easier to
swallow"; or grate it for very young children.

**Nutritionally it contributes nothing to speak of.** A USDA entry for cucumber
could not be verified at the time of writing, so this record publishes no iron
or vitamin C figure at all rather than a plausible-looking guess. Treat it as a
hydration and texture food. If you want iron, that is [[tofu]] or [[barley]]
cereal; if you want a vitamin-C partner, [[mango]] or [[green-peas]].

## Prep 6-8

Peel it. Then cut lengthways into narrow batons — long enough that an end sticks
out of a closed fist, and narrow rather than thick, so that if a piece does come
away it is a sliver and not a chunk. Cutting out the seedy core makes the batons
softer.

If judging thickness feels like guesswork, grate it instead. Grated peeled
cucumber is soft, wet, and short, and it stirs into [[yogurt]] or mixes with
other vegetables without any decision to make.

At this age most of what happens is mouthing and gumming rather than eating, and
that is fine — a cold cucumber baton is a pleasant thing to chew on.

## Prep 9-11

Same form: peeled, narrow batons, or grated. The pincer grasp arriving does not
change the geometry rule, and this is the age at which coins become tempting
because they are easy to pick up. They are the one shape to avoid.

## Prep 12-17

Still peeled and still batons rather than rounds. AAP's round-and-firm line runs
to four years, and cucumber is a firm raw vegetable for the whole of that
period.

Grated cucumber folded through plain yogurt is the easy household version and
works as a side for the whole table.

## Safety

**Never coins.** This is the single thing to take from this record. A raw
cucumber round is the geometry AAP explicitly warns about, and it is also the
way almost everyone cuts a cucumber.

**Peel it.** The skin does not break down under gums and holds a bitten piece
together. Peeling is a guideline-issued fix, not a nicety.

**Chilled cucumber for teething is common practice, not guidance.** Nobody
issues it. If you do it, the guideline-issued part is unchanged — peel it, cut a
baton — and the chilling is just comfort. A whole unpeeled chilled cucumber, or
a thick unpeeled spear, is not made safe by being cold.

Pickled cucumber is a different food: high sodium, and not appropriate before 24
months.
