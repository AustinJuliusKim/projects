## Background

A plantain is a cooking banana: starchier, firmer, and not eaten raw the way
[[banana]] is. Cooked ripe, it goes soft and sweet and holds its shape in the
hand, which makes it an easy food to give a baby who is learning to grip.

**Its useful number is vitamin C, at 20.1 mg per 100 g**, not iron — 0.32 mg per
100 g is a trace. That makes plantain a pairing food: served alongside
[[lentils]], [[black-beans]], [[teff]] or [[fortified-infant-cereal]], the
vitamin C improves absorption of the non-heme iron in the same meal, which is
CDC's own advice. On its own it is a pleasant carbohydrate and nothing more.

**Ripeness changes the food.** Green plantain is dense and starchy and stays
firm through boiling; a fully ripe one — skin mostly black — cooks soft in
minutes and tastes sweet. The ripeness stages have culinary names and rules of
thumb attached to them, but **no guideline body stages plantain ripeness**, so
what follows is the general rule applied honestly: cook it until it mashes
easily, whatever colour you started from, and ripe gets you there with less
work.

## Prep 6-8

Choose a ripe plantain — yellow going to black. Peel it, cut into
finger-length pieces about as thick as two of your fingers, and steam, boil or
bake until a piece gives way completely under light pressure. Roasted ripe
plantain is soft, sweet and holds together in a fist, which is close to ideal at
this age.

At this age the whole-hand grasp means a long piece with an end sticking out is
easier to manage than a small one. Small pieces are harder now, not safer.

**Not fried.** Tostones and fried maduros are firm, oily and usually salted, and
frying a green plantain and pressing it makes it harder rather than softer.

## Prep 9-11

Soft cubes about thumbnail size, with a couple of larger pieces alongside so
biting still gets practised. Mashed ripe plantain also works well as a base for
mashed beans or meat, which is the pairing that makes the vitamin C count.

## Prep 12-17

However the household eats it, kept soft and unsalted. Green plantain dishes are
fine here as long as they are cooked right through and mash easily; fried and
salted forms are still worth taking the baby's portion out of first.

## Safety

**Cook it until it mashes easily.** This is CDC's rule for hard fruits and
vegetables, and green plantain is firmly inside it — an undercooked piece of
green plantain is a hard food, not a soft one.

**No added sugar, no salt.** Ripe plantain is already sweet, and the common
preparations — fried in butter with sugar, or salted — both run into CDC's
advice to avoid added sugars before 24 months and to limit sodium.

Plantain is not one of the nine major US allergens. Cross-reactivity between
banana or plantain latex proteins and latex allergy is discussed in the research
literature, but **no infant-feeding guideline addresses it**, so this record
makes no claim about it in either direction.
