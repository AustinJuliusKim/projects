## Background

Toast is the most convenient way most families cover **wheat**, one of the nine
major US allergens, and it doubles as an early self-feeding food. Both of those
are true. The thing nobody mentions is the sodium.

**Wheat bread carries 473 mg of sodium per 100 g. Toasted, the same bread carries
601 mg.** Toasting does not add salt — it drives off water, which concentrates
everything left behind by roughly 27%, sodium included. A number like 601 mg per
100 g in a food routinely handed to six-month-olds as a first finger food
deserves a second look, and this record is giving it one. Buy the lowest-sodium
loaf you can find, or a no-salt-added one, and do not treat toast as a neutral
background food you can serve at every meal. If you want a low-sodium wheat
vehicle, that is [[wheat-pasta]], at 6 mg per 100 g.

**Be careful about what the numbers describe.** Both USDA entries behind this
record are for "**Bread, wheat**" — not whole-wheat. A verified entry for a
genuinely whole-wheat loaf could not be established while this was written, so
this record is labelled wheat, not whole-wheat, and you should not read its
figures onto a whole-grain loaf. The iron, 4.09 mg per 100 g, is **largely from
enrichment rather than from the grain**, which is why this record's iron is
tagged fortified.

**Wheat, gluten and celiac are three different things**, and they get muddled
constantly. Wheat is one of the nine major allergens and must be declared on a
label. Barley and rye contain gluten but are **not** major allergens — see
[[barley]], which covers that distinction and the celiac timing question in full.
"Gluten-free" and "wheat-free" are not the same claim. And celiac disease is not
an IgE allergy: it is not governed by allergen-introduction timing, and it is not
what this record's medical gate is about.

## Prep 6-8

Toast a slice **until it is dry the whole way through**, not just browned on the
outside. Press the middle with a finger: if it gives like fresh bread, it is not
there yet. The point is to remove the property that makes bread awkward — soft
crumb compresses into a dense, gummy wad against the palate, and a baby cannot
easily clear that.

Cut it into narrow strips, along the slice, **no more than about half an inch
wide** — AAP's half-inch rule is the only dimension any guideline body issues,
and here it applies to the width of the strip. Length is what lets a
six-month-old hold it with a whole-hand grasp and gnaw the protruding end. Crusts
on or off is your call; the crust is the part most likely to come off in a lump,
so trim it if it is thick.

One strip at a time on the tray. A handful of strips gets crammed.

Nothing on it, or a very thin scrape of something — see [[almond-butter]] or
[[tahini]] for how thinly, since a thick layer of any butter is separately a
hazard.

## Prep 9-11

Smaller pieces for the pincer grasp, still dry-toasted, still no bigger than
half an inch in any direction. This is also the age where soggy toast becomes a
problem in a new way: toast dipped in soup or milk and left to sit reverts to
the balled-up texture toasting was meant to fix. Serve it dipped and eaten, not
dipped and abandoned.

## Prep 12-17

However the family eats it, within the sodium caveat above. Sandwiches cut into
strips, toast with a thin spread, toast crumbs through other food. Keep watching
the loaf's label rather than the shape of the toast — by this age the shape has
stopped being the interesting part and the salt has not.

## Safety

**Bread is on no official choking list, and "toast it first" is issued by
nobody.** That is worth saying out loud. Toasting is sound, widespread practice
and this record recommends it, but it is common practice and not guidance, so it
is tiered that way. What *is* issued: AAP's half-inch maximum, NHS's narrow
batons, and CDC's principle of cutting cylindrical foods into short thin strips
rather than rounds. The strip shape here comes from those.

**Soft fresh bread is the form to avoid**, especially white sandwich bread torn
into pieces. It compresses instead of breaking up.

**Sodium is the ongoing risk, not the acute one.** 601 mg per 100 g toasted. CDC
advises against added salt for babies, and this record cannot give you a mg/day
infant limit because **no verified figure was available** — do not read that
silence as permission. Choose the lowest-sodium loaf available and vary the
grain: [[oatmeal]] and [[barley]] carry no salt at all.

**Wheat toast with a nut or seed butter is two hazard entries at once.** The
toast is fine; the thick glob is not. Spread it very thinly.
