## Background

Apple is one of the few foods in this canon that three separate guideline
bodies name by name — and all three name it as a hazard in its raw form. AAP
lists "raw fruit chunks, such as apple pieces" and says to keep them from
children "until 4 years of age or older." CDC's hazard list says "raw carrots or
apples." NHS covers it in its preparation guidance.

That is not a reason to skip apple. It is a reason to change its form, and
unusually there are **two fixes that guideline bodies actually issue** rather
than one someone extrapolated:

- **Cook it** until you can mash it easily with a fork (CDC and NHS both).
- **Grate it raw** (NHS).

Peel it either way. NHS notes that removing the skin makes food easier to
swallow, and apple skin does not break down under gums.

Nutritionally apple is close to filler: 0.12 mg of iron per 100 g raw, and
cooking destroys nearly all the vitamin C — USDA's cooked, peeled entry gives
0.2 mg per 100 g against 4.6 mg raw. So **cooked apple is not a useful vitamin-C
partner for an iron meal**; use [[mango]], [[green-peas]], or
[[butternut-squash]] for that. Apple's value here is that it is cheap,
year-round, familiar, and takes flavour well alongside things a baby is less
sure about.

No juice before 12 months. Applesauce is fine if it is unsweetened — added
sugars are off the menu under 24 months.

[[pear]] is handled differently because a ripe European pear goes soft;
[[korean-pear]] is handled like apple because it does not.

## Prep 6-8

Two routes, pick either.

**Cooked.** Peel, core, and slice. Simmer with a splash of water, or roast
covered, until a slice collapses under a fork — five to fifteen minutes
depending on thickness. Serve as finger-length slices, which is also what NHS
suggests for apple: "slices instead of small chunks." At this age the whole-hand
grasp means the piece is held in a fist with an end sticking out, so the baby
sets the bite size.

**Grated raw.** Peel, then grate on the coarse side of a box grater. The shreds
are soft enough to gum and short enough that no single piece can lodge. Stir
into yogurt or porridge, or offer on a loaded spoon.

## Prep 9-11

The pincer grasp arrives — AAP puts it at most often between 9 and 10 months —
so soft cooked cubes about the size of your thumbnail become pick-up-able. They
must still be cooked soft or grated. A pincer grip does not make a raw apple
cube safe; it just means the baby can now deliver one to their own airway.

Grated raw apple stays useful here and is the easier weekday version.

## Prep 12-17

Still cooked soft or grated raw. Baked apple, apple stirred through oatmeal,
apple sauce, apple in a stew.

Raw apple chunks and raw apple slices thick enough to snap off are the one thing
that does not change at twelve months. AAP's line is four years, not one.

## Safety

Raw apple is hard, smooth, and breaks into airway-sized wedges under a bite —
the exact combination that makes it one of the named entries on both the AAP and
CDC lists. A baby who can bite a piece off cannot reliably grind it, and the
piece that comes away is the wrong shape.

Cooking and grating are the two fixes, and they are guideline-issued rather than
inferred. Peeling is the third and applies to both.

Test cooked apple the way you would test any cooked fruit: press a piece between
your fingers. If it holds its shape, it needs longer.
