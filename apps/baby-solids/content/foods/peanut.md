## Background

The single most consequential food on this list. The LEAP trial randomized
infants at high risk to eat peanut or avoid it, and early, sustained
introduction reduced peanut allergy by roughly 81%. That result is why the
old "delay allergens" advice was reversed.

The part that gets lost: **introduction is not the finish line.** LEAP's
protocol wasn't a taste, it was about 6 g of peanut protein a week across
three or more feedings, sustained for years. CSACI's position statement is
blunt that occasional exposure may be worse than none — a single taste
followed by months of nothing can raise the risk of sensitization rather than
lower it. So this food has a maintenance schedule, not a checkbox.

## Prep 6-8

Stir about 1/4 teaspoon of smooth peanut butter into warm water, breast milk,
formula, or a puree the baby already eats, until it's thin enough to pour off
a spoon. Offer it in the morning, at home, on a day you're not rushing —
that's a logistics choice, not a medical one: it means you're watching, and
your clinic is open.

Watch for about two hours. IgE reactions happen within minutes to two hours,
which is also why the familiar "wait 3–5 days before the next new food" rule
adds nothing diagnostically — it just slows down variety.

## Prep 9-11

Same thinned form. Thickness, not quantity, is the hazard here: nut butter
stiff enough to hold its shape can obstruct an airway.

## Prep 12-17

Still thinned or spread thinly — on a strip of toast, stirred into oatmeal or
yoghurt. The maintenance target doesn't change: keep it in the rotation at
least three times a week rather than letting weeks lapse.

## Safety

Two separate risks, often confused.

**Allergy** is managed by early, regular exposure — with the medical gate
above for babies with severe eczema or egg allergy.

**Choking** is managed by form. Whole peanuts and tree nuts are firm, round,
and airway-sized: AAP keeps them off the list until age 4. A spoonful of thick
peanut butter is on the same list. Thinned or spread very thinly, peanut is
fine from six months.
