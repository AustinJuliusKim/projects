## Background

Both CDC and NHS name peas among early vegetables, and they are one of the few
foods on this list that pair themselves: 1.54 mg of iron and 14.2 mg of vitamin
C per 100 g cooked, in the same spoonful. Vitamin C improves absorption of plant
(non-heme) iron eaten in the same meal, so the pea does the pairing work that
[[spinach]] or [[tofu]] need a second food for.

Frozen peas are fine and often better than fresh — they are picked and frozen
quickly, and they cook in minutes. Raw peas hold more vitamin C (40 mg per 100 g
against 14.2 cooked) but are firm, so cooking is the trade you make.

## Prep 6-8

Simmer or steam until a pea flattens when you press it. Then smash them —
against the side of a bowl with a fork, or stirred into a thicker purée such as
[[butternut-squash]] so they have something to hold onto.

Loose peas at this age mostly end up on the floor. That is not a safety problem,
it is a physics one: a whole-hand grasp cannot pick up a sphere. Loading a spoon
and handing it over, or spreading smashed peas on a strip of toast, gets more
into the baby.

## Prep 9-11

The pincer grasp — AAP puts it at most often between 9 and 10 months — is
exactly the tool a pea is shaped for, and picking them up one at a time is good
practice. Many parents keep squashing each pea between finger and thumb first.

Be clear about what that habit is. **No AAP or CDC document names green peas as
a choking hazard.** AAP's rule concerns round *firm* food — "do not feed
children younger than 4 round, firm food unless it is chopped completely" — and
a properly cooked pea is soft, not firm. Smashing is a conservative
extrapolation from that rule rather than an instruction anyone issued. It costs
nothing, so it is a reasonable thing to do; it is not a thing you have failed to
do if you skip it.

## Prep 12-17

Whole, cooked, however the rest of the table is eating them. Mushy peas, peas
stirred through rice, peas in a soup.

## Safety

The real failure mode is undercooking. A pea straight from the freezer, or one
blanched for thirty seconds, stays firm — that is when AAP's round-and-firm rule
genuinely applies. Cook until they give.

Dried whole peas and split peas are a different food and need cooking to a soft
mash; CDC lists whole beans as a choking hazard, and a hard dried pulse belongs
in that group.
