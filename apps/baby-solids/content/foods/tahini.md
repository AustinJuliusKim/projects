## Background

Tahini is the best vehicle in this canon for **sesame**, the ninth major US
allergen: it is the highest-protein-density, lowest-choking-risk form the seed
comes in, and it mixes into food a baby already eats without changing the
texture much.

Sesame became a major allergen late. The FASTER Act was signed on **23 April
2021** and took effect **1 January 2023**, so any jar bought before 2023, and a
great deal of parenting advice written before then, predates mandatory sesame
declaration. If you are reading an older label or an older article, it was
written under different rules.

**This record publishes no iron figure and no USDA id, and the reason is
interesting rather than merely cautious.** Sesame seeds are one of the most
iron- and calcium-dense foods in this canon — whole seeds run about 14.6 mg iron
and 975 mg calcium per 100 g, roasted whole about 14.8 and 989. But
**decortication changes them enormously**: hulled kernels drop to 7.78 mg iron
and **131 mg calcium**, a loss of roughly 47% of the iron and **about 87% of the
calcium**. Most commercial tahini is made from hulled kernels, so tahini tracks
the kernel row rather than the whole-seed row — which means the "sesame is a
calcium powerhouse" line you may have read is describing a food most jars are
not. **The tahini value itself is unverified**, so no number appears on this
record. If you want the whole-seed profile, that is whole sesame seed, finely
ground, not tahini.

## Prep 6-8

**Stir the jar first, properly.** Tahini separates hard: a layer of oil on top
and a dense, near-solid paste underneath, and if you skim off the top you are
serving thin oil while the concentrated paste waits for the next spoon. Take it
down to the bottom of the jar with a sturdy spoon or a butter knife and bring it
back to one uniform consistency. A jar stored upside down for a day makes this
easier.

**Then thin it further.** The stirred paste is still thick, and the oil layer is
not thinning — it is part of the food that got separated from itself. Add warm
water, breast milk, formula, or a familiar puree a little at a time, and stir
until **it no longer holds its shape and pours off the spoon.**

**There is no issued dilution ratio.** The specific one in circulation for nut
and seed butters is issued by nobody. AAP's rule is qualitative — spread thinly.
CDC names the hazard and gives no fix. The one numeric preparation anywhere in US
guidance belongs to NIAID's peanut-allergy-prevention protocol: **written for
peanut**, a dosing instruction rather than a choking rule, and it does not
transfer to sesame. A borrowed number would be the wrong ratio applied to the
wrong food. Thin until it pours.

Offer it in the morning, at home, on a day you are not rushing, and watch for
about two hours.

## Prep 9-11

Same thinned form, stirred through [[yogurt]], [[oatmeal]], mashed
[[sweet-potato]], or a bean mash. **Thickness, not quantity, is the hazard.**

Hummus is a reasonable route at this age if it is smooth and unsalted, but read
it as a chickpea food that contains tahini rather than as a sesame serving —
the amount of sesame in a spoonful of hummus is small and variable, and if you
are deliberately introducing the allergen, tahini itself is the honest vehicle.

## Prep 12-17

Thinned into sauces and dressings, stirred through vegetables, spread very
thinly on [[wheat-toast]]. Still never off a spoon, still never a thick layer.

Whole sesame seeds appear on a lot of things at this age — bread crusts, bagels,
banchan, dressings. Whole seeds are inside CDC's "whole or chopped nuts and
**seeds**" entry, and NHS's instruction for the seed form is to serve them
finely ground. A scatter of seeds baked into a crust is a different case from a
spoonful of loose seeds; the loose seeds are the ones to grind or skip.

## Safety

**Tahini is on the hazard list, not near it.** CDC's entry reads "chunks or
spoonful of nut and **seed** butters." The word "seed" is doing real work there:
parents often read the nut-butter warnings as not applying to tahini, and they
do apply, by name.

**The separation is a safety issue, not a texture one.** Unstirred tahini at the
bottom of a jar is stiffer than peanut butter. Stir, then thin.

**Never off a spoon, never a thick glob**, for the same physical reason as the
nut butters: a paste stiff enough to hold its shape can obstruct an airway.

**Sesame oil does not do this job.** If you are trying to introduce sesame, use
tahini or finely ground sesame seed — see [[sesame-oil]] for why an oil is not
an introduction vehicle.

**A note on the form gap.** NHS issues "serve seeds finely ground." The US has
no equivalent form instruction for sesame despite sesame now being a major
allergen. That gap is real, and this record leans on the NHS wording because
nothing domestic fills it.
