## Background

**The highest non-heme iron of any whole food in this batch — 3.33 mg per 100 g
cooked**, plus 9.02 g of protein, from something that costs almost nothing and
cooks in half an hour without soaking. Both NHS and the Canadian Paediatric
Society name lentils for this age.

Non-heme iron is absorbed less readily than the heme iron in [[beef]] or
[[salmon]], and CDC's answer is to serve it with a vitamin-C food. Lentils bring
only 1.5 mg of vitamin C themselves, so pair them — [[broccoli]] is one CDC
names by name. A dal with a green vegetable stirred through it is doing real
nutritional work, not just filling a bowl.

## Prep 6-8

Rinse, then simmer in unsalted water or broth until they lose their shape
entirely. Red and split lentils fall apart on their own in about twenty minutes;
brown, green, and Puy lentils hold together much longer and need either more
time or a mash at the end.

Serve as a thick puree off a loaded spoon, or spread on a strip of toast. No
salt — high-sodium foods are advised against under two years.

## Prep 9-11

Thicker and lumpier is fine now, and lentils are a good carrier for iron-rich
company: stir in finely chopped [[chicken-thigh]] or mix with [[oatmeal]]. Mash
any lentils that are still visibly whole.

## Prep 12-17

However the family eats them — dal, soup, stirred through rice. Softness is
still the test.

## Safety

Here is an honest ambiguity rather than a resolved rule. **Lentils are not
individually named on CDC's choking-hazard list, but "whole beans" is**, and
lentils are pulses. Nobody has published a ruling on whether a cooked lentil
falls inside that entry.

What is clear is the physical difference: a cooked black bean stays a smooth
round object, while a cooked red or split lentil has usually already
disintegrated. This record treats lentils as needing modification — cook them
down, mash what is left round — which costs nothing and stays on the right side
of an entry that may or may not include them. Whole firm lentils, the kind that
survive a salad, are the form to avoid.

No salt, for the same reason as everywhere else in the first two years. Lentils
are not one of the nine major US allergens.
