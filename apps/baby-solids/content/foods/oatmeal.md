## Background

**The fortification is the entire point, and it is easy to lose by accident.**
Fortified infant oat cereal carries 64.1 mg of iron per 100 g dry. Plain oatmeal
cooked in water, unenriched, carries 0.90 mg. That is roughly a seventyfold gap
between two things a parent would reasonably both call "oatmeal".

So: **a pot of homemade porridge is not a nutritional substitute for fortified
infant cereal.** It is a fine food and a fine texture. It is not doing the iron
job, and if it quietly replaces the fortified box, the iron in the diet drops by
almost all of it. If you prefer cooking your own oats, keep an iron-rich food
somewhere else in the day — see [[beef]] or [[lentils]].

The iron in fortified cereal is added, non-heme iron, not the heme iron in meat.
CDC's advice to serve a vitamin-C food alongside non-heme iron applies here:
[[broccoli]] is one CDC names by name.

**Why oat rather than rice.** CDC's own wording is to "offer a variety of
fortified infant cereals such as oat, barley, and multi-grain instead of only
rice cereal," and AAP notes rice cereal does not have to be the first cereal.
The concern behind that is inorganic arsenic, which concentrates in rice: FDA
has set an
action level of 100 ppb for infant rice cereal and notes that infants' rice
intake relative to body weight is about three times an adult's. Oat sidesteps
that without giving anything up.

## Prep 6-8

Follow the box, then follow CDC: mix the cereal with breast milk, formula, or
water until it is smooth. Start thin — closer to a pourable consistency than a
paste — and thicken it over the following weeks as your baby gets better at
moving food to the back of the mouth.

Load the spoon and hand it over, or let them take it off a loaded spoon you hold.
Either way most of the first weeks land on the tray and the chin. That is the
job at this age.

## Prep 9-11

Thicker, and increasingly self-fed. This is also the point where oatmeal earns
its keep as a carrier: mashed [[black-beans]], flaked [[salmon]], or fruit stir
into it and go down easily.

## Prep 12-17

However the family eats it. If you have moved to rolled or steel-cut oats cooked
from scratch, keep the smoothness rule in view — CDC's instruction is to cook and
then finely grind or mash whole-grain kernels, and steel-cut oats are kernels.

## Safety

Smooth porridge is a low-risk texture. **Whole cooked grain kernels are not** —
they are a named entry on CDC's choking-hazard list, and CDC's instruction is to
cook and then finely grind or mash them. Instant and quick oats break down on
their own; steel-cut and whole groats do not, so blend or mash those.

Oats are not one of the nine major US allergens. Oat products are sometimes
processed on shared lines with wheat, but no infant-feeding guideline addresses
that question, so this record makes no claim about it in either direction — if
wheat matters in your household, read the label.
