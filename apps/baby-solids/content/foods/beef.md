## Background

Beef is the clearest example of why the old "food before one is just for fun"
line does not hold. WHO's 2023 complementary feeding guideline recommends
animal-source foods — meat, fish, or eggs — **daily** from six months as a
strong recommendation, and Health Canada and the Canadian Paediatric Society ask
for iron-rich foods from about six months, at least twice a day.

What makes beef different from the plant sources is the *form* of the iron. CDC
classifies the iron in meat, poultry, and fish as heme iron and says plainly
that it is more easily absorbed than the non-heme iron in plants and fortified
cereals. Pan-browned ground beef carries 2.93 mg per 100 g, plus zinc, and
unlike [[lentils]] or [[black-beans]] it does not need a vitamin-C food beside
it — heme absorption is not vitamin-C dependent. Pair it with [[broccoli]] if
you like, but for taste, not chemistry.

CPS names "minced" among suitable forms, which lines up with the hazard list:
ground beef is the guideline-friendliest way to serve it.

## Prep 6-8

Buy ground, or grind it yourself. Brown it through, breaking it up finer than
you would for yourself, then stir in enough of its own juices, unsalted broth,
or a familiar puree that it clumps instead of scattering. Loose dry mince is
genuinely hard for a six-month-old to collect — it goes everywhere and nothing
gets eaten.

Cook ground beef to 160 °F (USDA FSIS). Remove any fat or gristle **before**
cooking rather than picking it out afterwards; that is CDC's instruction and it
is easier that way.

A common alternative at this age is a large, palm-sized patty or a strip of
slow-cooked beef the baby gnaws on without detaching pieces. That is widespread
practice, not a guideline instruction, and it depends on the meat being soft
enough to shred under pressure. If it holds together when you pull at it, it is
the wrong piece.

## Prep 9-11

Same ground form, and now easier — a pincer grasp picks up mince that a whole
hand could only push around. Mixed into [[oatmeal]] or mashed vegetables it goes
down faster than on its own.

## Prep 12-17

Whatever the table is eating, subject to two things. Meat still needs to be soft
and cut small: "tough or large chunks of meat" stays on CDC's hazard list past
the first birthday. And processed beef stays out — hot dogs, sausages, and lunch
meat are excluded twice over, once for sodium and once because CDC's rule for
cylindrical foods is to cut them into short thin strips rather than rounds.

## Safety

Two separate things, both real.

**Choking.** Chunks of meat are a named hazard-list entry. Ground, finely
minced, or shredded-to-collapse is the safe form; a cube of steak is not, at any
age in this range.

**Cooking.** Ground beef to 160 °F, whole cuts to 145 °F with a three-minute
rest. Ground meat mixes surface bacteria all the way through, which is why its
target is higher than a steak's — for a baby that margin is not worth trimming.
