## Background

Walnut is the third distinct tree-nut sensitization in this canon, after
[[almond-butter]] and [[cashew-butter]]. Tolerating one tree nut says nothing
about another; walnut's reported clustering is with hazelnut, not with almond or
cashew.

**Read this before anything else: "chopped fine" is not a safe form.** CDC's
choking-hazard entry is "**whole or chopped** nuts and seeds." Chopping is
*inside* the hazard entry. It is not a mitigation, not a compromise, and not a
smaller version of a hazard — it is the hazard, listed by name, alongside the
whole nut. This matters because "finely chopped" sounds careful. It sounds like
the responsible thing a parent does with a nut. It is not, and a chopped walnut
fragment is a hard, irregular, airway-sized piece with the density to lodge.

**The only sanctioned form is finely ground.** That is NHS's instruction —
serve them finely ground — and it is the single positive form-instruction any
body issues for nuts. Ground means powder or paste: the walnut has stopped being
pieces. Then stir it into something moist so it cannot be inhaled as loose dust.

**Every age band in this record is the same form**, which is unusual in this
canon and deliberate. No band gets a piece of walnut. Nine months does not earn
one, and neither does eighteen. The bands differ in what you stir it into, not
in how coarse it can be.

This is one of the few foods here that is genuinely useful nutritionally in the
same breath: ground walnut is a real non-heme iron contributor at **2.91 mg per
100 g**, and the USDA id behind that figure is for whole English walnuts, which
is the exact food — ground walnut is walnut, ground. Pair it with a vitamin-C
food in the same meal, since non-heme iron absorbs poorly on its own:
[[mango]], [[broccoli]], [[butternut-squash]].

## Prep 6-8

Grind walnuts in a spice grinder, a small food processor, or a mortar, and keep
going past the point where it looks done. You want a powder, or a paste — walnut
is oily enough that a fine grind will start clumping into a butter, which is
fine. **If you can see distinct pieces, it is not ground yet.** Pass it through
your fingers: no grit you can feel as an individual fragment.

Stir a quarter teaspoon through something moist — [[oatmeal]], [[yogurt]],
mashed [[banana]], a puree. The moisture matters for a second reason besides
taste: dry nut powder on a tray is inhalable, and a baby who breathes in over a
spoonful of dust has a different problem from a choking one.

Offer it in the morning, at home, on a day you are not rushing, and watch for
about two hours. IgE reactions happen within minutes to two hours.

## Prep 9-11

Still finely ground. The pincer grasp arriving is the reason parents start
offering small pieces of other foods around now, and it is exactly the wrong
instinct here — the pincer grasp lets a baby pick up a nut fragment, it does not
give them the molars or the coordination to deal with one.

Grind a batch and keep it in the freezer; it goes rancid fast at room
temperature. Stir it through porridge, mashed vegetables, a thick soup.

## Prep 12-17

Still finely ground, folded into whatever the table is eating — baked into
things, stirred through sauces, blended into a dip. A twelve-month-old is not
closer to handling a walnut half than a six-month-old is.

Watch for walnut arriving as pieces in adult food at this age: banchan, salads,
brownies, baklava, muesli, walnut-topped breads. That is where the accidental
first piece usually comes from, not from a bowl of nuts.

## Safety

**This is the highest-risk food in this batch, and the risk is entirely about
form.**

**Chopped is a hazard, not a mitigation.** Saying it again because it is the one
thing people get wrong: CDC lists "whole or **chopped** nuts and seeds." A
recipe that says "finely chopped walnuts" has not made the food safe for a baby.
Grind it.

**Whole nuts have a hard age floor, and the bodies disagree on where it is.**
NIAID: whole nuts should not be given to children **less than 5 years** of age.
AAP's general framing is "until **4 years** of age or older." Both positions are
current. **NIAID's five-year floor is the more cautious default and is the one
this record encodes**, which is why the restriction on this record reads 60
months. This record surfaces the disagreement rather than picking one and
pretending it is unanimous — if your pediatrician works to the four-year line,
that is a defensible position with AAP behind it.

**Ground is not restricted.** The 60-month floor is about whole and chopped
nuts. Finely ground walnut stirred into a moist food is appropriate from six
months, and that is the whole point of this record existing.

**Nut butters are a separate hazard entry with a separate rule.** If you make
walnut butter by grinding far enough, treat it like [[almond-butter]]: smooth,
thinned until it pours, never a spoonful, never a thick glob.
