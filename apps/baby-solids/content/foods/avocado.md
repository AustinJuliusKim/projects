## Background

One of the few foods that needs no cooking and no softening — a ripe avocado
is already at the texture everything else has to be cooked down to. It appears
on no choking-hazard list, and no primary source restricts it.

Do not think of it as an iron food. At 0.55 mg per 100 g of non-heme iron it
contributes very little, so it belongs alongside the foods doing the real work —
[[beef]], fortified [[oatmeal]], [[lentils]] — rather than instead of them. It is
mostly fat and calories, which at this age is a point in its favour.

Related: [[banana]] is the other no-cook soft food and behaves much the same in
the hand.

## Prep 6-8

Halve, remove the pit, and cut the flesh into long wedges — roughly the length
of an adult finger, thick enough not to collapse when squeezed. Leaving a
strip of skin along the back gives something dry to hold; the skin itself is
not eaten.

One honest caveat about that shape: AAP's published rule is to cut food for
infants into pieces no larger than one-half inch, and that is the only
dimensional instruction any US body issues. Longer soft-food spears are
widespread practice rather than a guideline instruction, and they rest on the
food yielding under light pressure. If a piece holds its shape when you press
it, it is the wrong piece regardless of its length.

## Prep 9-11

Once your baby picks things up between thumb and forefinger instead of raking
at them, small cubes work and are less frustrating. Keep offering a wedge or
two alongside — biting off a piece is a separate skill from picking one up.

## Prep 12-17

However the rest of the table is eating it: cubed, sliced, mashed on toast,
stirred through other foods. No caveat beyond ripeness.

## Safety

Ripeness is the only real variable. A hard avocado is a firm raw food and
should be left to ripen rather than served, which is the same logic CDC
applies to hard raw fruit and vegetables generally.

Some people with latex allergy react to avocado, but no primary infant-feeding
source addresses that, so this record makes no claim about it either way.
