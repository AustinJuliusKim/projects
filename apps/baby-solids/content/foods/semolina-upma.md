## Background

Upma is a savoury semolina porridge, and semolina is wheat — so this is a way to
cover the **wheat** allergen inside a dish the household may already cook, rather
than through [[wheat-toast]] or [[wheat-pasta]].

**The porridge is easy. The tempering is where the problems are.** A traditional
tadka goes into hot oil at the start: mustard seed, curry leaf, urad or chana
dal, cashew, sometimes peanut. That short list contains **two more of the nine US
major allergens** — tree nut and peanut — plus mustard, which is a priority
allergen in the UK and EU but is **not** one of the US nine and carries no
US labelling requirement. For a baby's first exposure to wheat, that is three or
four allergens arriving in one bowl, and if something happens you will not know
which one did it.

**And it is the choking hazard too, not the porridge.** Whole mustard seeds pop
and stay whole. Cashews go in as halves and stay as halves. Both sit squarely
inside CDC's "whole or chopped nuts and seeds" entry. The semolina itself, cooked
smooth and thin, is one of the lowest-risk textures in this canon — all of the
risk in this dish is floating in it.

So: **temper plainly, grind the solids in, or leave the tadka out.** The dish is
still upma without it, and you can add it back for the rest of the family at the
table.

**This record publishes no iron figure and no USDA id, and the gap matters more
here than elsewhere.** Enriched and unenriched semolina differ enormously in
iron — enrichment is where most of the iron in a Western box of semolina comes
from — and no verified entry could be established for either while this was
written. That is not a rounding difference; it materially changes what the food
is for. Read the box: if it says enriched, there is meaningful added iron in
there, and this record simply cannot tell you how much. If you need an iron food
you can count, use [[barley]] cereal, [[lentils]] or [[beef]].

## Prep 6-8

Dry-roast the semolina in a pan for a few minutes until it smells nutty and
stops looking chalky — that step is what keeps it from going gluey. Then whisk
it into hot water, breast milk, or formula off the heat, and cook it out until
it is **smooth and thin**, thinner than you would serve an adult, which is CDC's
texture instruction for this age.

**No salt.** Upma is a savoury dish and salt is the default in every recipe you
will find; leave it out entirely for a baby. Lemon juice, a little grated
vegetable cooked in soft, or a pinch of ground spice carry the flavour instead.

**No tempering for a first wheat exposure.** If you want some of that character,
warm a little oil, add ground spices only — no whole seeds, no nuts — and stir
it through.

Offer it in the morning, at home, on a day you are not rushing, and watch for
about two hours.

## Prep 9-11

Thicker, with soft cooked vegetables grated or mashed into it — [[carrot]],
[[green-peas]], [[butternut-squash]]. This is a good dish for the transition to
lumpier textures because the base carries the lumps rather than leaving them
loose on a tray.

If you want the tadka now, grind it: mustard seed ground to a powder is a spice
rather than a seed, and cashew ground fine is [[cashew-butter]] by another name.
Whole seeds and nut pieces stay out.

## Prep 12-17

Fluffier upma with vegetables through it, still unsalted or barely salted, still
with the tempering ground or omitted. The whole-nut floor does not move at this
age — NIAID puts whole nuts at under 5 years and AAP at 4 years or older, and a
cashew half in a bowl of upma is a whole nut.

## Safety

**The tempering, not the porridge.** Whole mustard seeds and cashew halves are
inside CDC's "whole or chopped nuts and seeds" entry. Grind them or omit them.
Curry leaf is soft and not a choking concern, but it is also not something a
baby will chew — fish it out or chop it very fine.

**Three extra allergens can arrive unannounced.** Cashew (tree nut), peanut, and
mustard. Two are among the US nine; mustard is not, which means a US label is not
obliged to flag it even though a mustard allergy is real and is recognised as a
priority allergen in the UK and EU. If you are eating someone else's upma, ask
what went into the tadka.

**No salt.** CDC advises against added salt for babies, and this record cannot
give you an infant mg/day limit because **no verified figure was available** —
that silence is not permission.

**Semolina is wheat, not a separate grain.** If wheat is being avoided, upma is
out. And "gluten-free" is a different claim from "wheat-free" — see [[barley]].
