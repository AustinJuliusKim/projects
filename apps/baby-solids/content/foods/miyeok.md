## Background

미역국 is the soup a Korean mother eats after giving birth and the soup the
family eats on birthdays. It is going to be in the house, and a baby is going to
be offered some. This page is about what is actually known — which is less than
you would expect, and we are going to be straight about that rather than dress
it up.

🚨 **The primary literature on seaweed iodine disagrees with itself by up to two
orders of magnitude.** Not a small measurement discrepancy. Two peer-reviewed
studies, both credible, roughly 50 to 150 times apart:

- **Dawczynski and colleagues (2007)**, using ICP-MS on 34 dried algae products,
  found brown algae containing **as much as 1,316 ± 1,669 mg of iodine per kg of
  fresh matter**, with more than **4,000 mg/kg** in several *Laminaria* species.
- **González and colleagues (2020)**, using redox titration on 30 samples,
  reported **European kombu at 27.7 ± 5.4 mg/kg dry weight**, and calculated
  that 4 g a day would supply about 111 µg of iodine.

Different methods, different sample origins, different answers. **This record
reports both and does not average them, and does not pick one.** An average of
two numbers that far apart would be a fabricated third number with no source
behind it, and picking the low one would be optimism dressed as evidence.

⚠️ **Neither figure is actually about 미역.** Dawczynski's number is a group mean
across three genera of brown algae; there was **no 미역-specific figure we could
verify at all.** Attributing a brown-algae group mean to 미역 alone would be
invention, so we have not done it.

**Nobody has written the guidance you are looking for.** No guideline body we
checked — AAP, CDC, NHS, Health Canada, ESPGHAN, EFSA — **addresses 미역국 for
infants at all.** Not to permit it, not to restrict it, not to set a frequency.
That is a genuine gap in the literature and not something we are going to paper
over with a confident-sounding rule.

Here is what *is* established, and it is enough to act on:

- **There is no upper intake level for iodine in infants.** NIH ODS lists the UL
  for both 0–6 and 7–12 months as **"not possible to establish."** An absence of
  a limit, never a limit that was withdrawn — and not permission.
- The nearest real ceiling is the **200 µg UL at ages 1 to 3.** Applying it to an
  eleven-month-old is inference, not guideline.
- Adequate intake is **110 µg/day (0–6 months)** and **130 µg/day (7–12
  months)**.
- Iodine content across commercial seaweeds spans **16 µg/g to 2,984 µg/g** — a
  186-fold range. **You cannot read the dose off a package.**
- Too much iodine produces some of the same effects as too little: goiter,
  elevated TSH, hypothyroidism, and it is also associated with thyroiditis.

**Our inference, clearly labelled as ours:** occasional 미역국 with the seaweed
strained out is a very different exposure from a daily bowl with the seaweed
eaten, and a daily bowl is worth raising with your pediatrician. No body issues
that. It follows from the facts above, and we think it is the sensible reading,
but you should weigh it knowing where it came from.

### 다시마 (kombu) is the real outlier, and families use them interchangeably

This matters because 다시마 usually shares a shelf with 미역, both go into stock,
and it is natural to assume they behave alike. **They do not.**

⚠️ Working across two sources with different methods: Miyai's kombu contained
**35 mg of iodine in 15 g**, about **2,333 µg/g**, against roughly **23 µg/g**
for dried nori — **on the order of a hundredfold difference.** Treat that as an
order-of-magnitude statement rather than a precise ratio; the arithmetic is
ours, spanning two studies that did not use the same method.

And 다시마 is the only seaweed here with a **human trial** behind the concern.
**Miyai and colleagues (2008)** gave normal Japanese adults **15 and 30 g of
kombu daily** and saw serum **TSH rise significantly, exceeding normal limits in
some subjects.** Fifteen grams a day for 55 to 87 days kept TSH elevated; values
returned to baseline 7 to 40 days after stopping. That is ordinary culinary
quantities, in healthy adults, producing measurable thyroid suppression.

**Our inference again:** do not let a baby drink 다시마 stock routinely, strain
the kelp out, and do not reduce the stock — reducing concentrates the iodine
along with the flavour.

## Prep 6-8

**The broth, with the seaweed strained out, is the gentler starting point.**
Soak dried 미역 until it swells — it expands a great deal, so use much less than
looks right — rinse it well, and simmer it into a plain unsalted soup with beef
or nothing at all. Strain the fronds out and offer a few spoons of the broth,
or use the broth to moisten rice or [[oatmeal]].

**If you are serving the seaweed itself, chop it very short.** Rehydrated 미역 is
long, slippery and slithery, and a strand does not break cleanly under a baby's
bite the way a soft vegetable does. Chop it far shorter than you think you need
to — well under half an inch, AAP's limit.

**No 간장, no salt, no 국간장.** 미역국 is normally seasoned with soy sauce or
salt; take the baby's portion before that happens.

**Occasional, not routine.** That is the frequency guidance, and it is
deliberately not a number, because no number exists to give you.

## Prep 9-11

The same, with slightly more of the seaweed itself if the baby is managing it —
still chopped short, still unsalted, still occasional. A pincer grasp handles
short chopped fronds well; long strands remain a bad idea regardless of hand
skill, because the hazard is the strand, not the picking-up.

## Prep 12-17

미역국 as the family eats it, lightly seasoned or unseasoned, with the fronds
chopped. Frequency is still the open question — a toddler now has a 200 µg
iodine upper limit to reason against, which is more than an infant had, but a
bowl of soup whose iodine content is unknowable within two orders of magnitude
is still not an everyday food.

Keep 다시마 stock out of the child's cup as a drink, whatever their age.

## Safety

**Iodine, and the honest position is that it is unquantifiable per package.**
The literature disagrees by up to two orders of magnitude, no 미역-specific
figure exists, and commercial seaweed spans a 186-fold range. Treat seaweed
iodine as **unknowable from the label** — that is the cautious default.

**There is no infant upper limit to check against.** "Not possible to establish"
for both infant bands. That is an absence of a limit, not a clearance, and this
record does not convert it into a permission or into an invented maximum. You
will find no maximum-per-week figure here for that reason.

**No guideline body addresses 미역국 for infants.** Every frequency and portion
statement on this page is our inference from the iodine facts, and is labelled
where it appears.

**다시마 is not interchangeable with 미역** — roughly a hundredfold more iodine by
the arithmetic above, and the only one of these seaweeds with a human trial
showing thyroid suppression at normal culinary amounts. Strain it, do not reduce
the stock, and do not serve it as a drink.

**Strands are a choking consideration.** Rehydrated fronds are long and
slippery. Chop them short. No body names this hazard for seaweed specifically —
it is our caution, applied from the general principle, not an issued rule.

**Sodium.** 미역국 is normally salted or seasoned with 국간장. Pull the baby's
portion before seasoning.

**Excess iodine looks like deficiency** — goiter, elevated TSH, hypothyroidism.
If a baby is eating seaweed regularly and you are unsure, that is a
pediatrician conversation, not a thing to resolve from a package label. See
[[gim]] for the same issue at a scale that is at least measurable.
