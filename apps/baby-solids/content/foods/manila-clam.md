## Background

바지락 turns up in more Korean home cooking than almost any other shellfish — in
칼국수, in soups, in 된장찌개 — so for a lot of families the question is not
whether to serve it but how to get a baby's portion out of a pot that was made
for adults.

**The defining prep problem is grit, not nutrition.** A clam that has not been
purged carries sand, and sand in a shellfish stew is unpleasant for an adult and
genuinely off-putting to a baby who is still deciding whether they like this
food at all. Purging in salted water is the standard fix. It is **ordinary
practice** — no guideline body addresses it, and it is a palatability step
rather than a safety one.

**The iron figure you were expecting is not here, on purpose.** Clams are
conventionally listed among the densest iron foods there are. We could not
verify that: the USDA database was unavailable when this record was researched,
and NIH's iron fact sheet does not list clams at all. This is exactly the case
where a well-known number is most tempting to supply from memory and most
important not to. What we will state is the classification — shellfish flesh
carries **heme** iron, the more readily absorbed form — and that the quantity is
pending verification. If you need a verified iron figure today, [[oyster]] and
[[beef]] have one.

**Iodine, with a caveat attached to the number.** USDA's iodine database gives
**66.5 µg per 100 g** — but that entry is for **canned, mixed-species clams**,
not fresh Manila clams. It is a proxy, and it is labelled as one here because
species and preparation both move iodine content around. It is enough to say
clams are a moderate iodine food: well above [[scallop]], well below the
seaweeds, and not a reason to avoid them.

**On allergy, the same split that catches everyone with molluscs.** Clams are
molluscs, and molluscs are **not** among the nine US major allergens — that list
names **crustacean** shellfish (crab, lobster, shrimp), not clam, oyster, mussel
or scallop. FDA's January 2025 final guidance sets out how it handles allergens
outside the nine. So: mollusc allergy is real, but **a US label need not warn
about it**, and "Contains: shellfish" refers to crustacean. Read the ingredient
list rather than the allergen line. And a shrimp reaction does not by itself
predict a clam reaction.

## Prep 6-8

**Purge first.** Put the clams in cool salted water for an hour or so somewhere
dark and let them spit out their sand, then lift them out rather than pouring
them out — the grit is now at the bottom of the bowl and you do not want to pour
it back over them. Rinse.

**Discard any clam whose shell is already open before cooking.** That is CDC's
rule, and it is stricter than FDA's, which is why it is the one here.

**Cook by boiling until the shells open, then keep boiling another 3 to 5
minutes.** Steaming works too, 4 to 9 minutes. **Throw away any that do not open
fully.**

Then the taking-apart, which matters more than the cooking:

- **Feel through every clam for shell chips.** Clam shells are brittle and
  fragments end up in the meat.
- **Cut off the tough siphon tip** — the dark rubbery neck. It does not soften.
- **Chop the rest fine and moisten** it with a spoonful of the cooking broth so
  it clumps instead of scattering across the tray.
- **Nothing larger than half an inch** — AAP's rule and the only dimensional
  rule any US guideline body issues.

Use the broth sparingly and unsalted. Clam liquor is naturally salty before
anyone adds anything.

## Prep 9-11

Same purge, same cooking, same shell check. Pieces up to half an inch suit the
pincer grasp that most babies develop around nine to ten months. Clams mixed
into soft rice or [[oatmeal]] are easier to gather than loose pieces, and the
broth carries the flavour into the rest of the bowl.

## Prep 12-17

However the household eats them, with the baby's share lifted out **before** the
soup is seasoned. This is the practical constraint on 바지락 for a toddler:
칼국수 broth, 된장 and the salt that goes into most clam dishes carry far more
sodium than a small child's day should, and you cannot dilute your way out of
it once it is in the pot.

Never the shells to play with. The broken edges are sharp.

## Safety

**Raw or barely-opened clams are out at every age.** FDA names children among
the groups who should avoid raw or undercooked shellfish. "Just opened" is not
cooked — CDC's 3-to-5-minutes-past-opening is the standard to hit.

**Shell chips and the siphon tip** are the two things to remove by hand. The
meat itself is soft once cooked; those two are not.

**Mercury is not a concern.** FDA and EPA place clams in **Best Choices**, and
on the sub-list of choices that are *even lower* in mercury.

**Sodium is the constraint families actually run into**, because clams are
almost always cooked into something salted. Pull the baby's portion before
seasoning — a sound and widely given instruction, but one that traces to no
numbered rule, so treat it as practice rather than a guideline.

**No guideline body addresses Manila clam as an infant food.** The cooking
rules, the mercury rating and the half-inch limit are issued. The purge, the
siphon trim, the shell check and the chopping are category rules plus ordinary
practice, applied here by us.
