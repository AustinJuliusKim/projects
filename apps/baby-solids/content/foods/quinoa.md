## Background

Quinoa is a reasonable everyday grain for a baby: 1.49 mg of iron per 100 g
cooked, plus protein and fibre, and it cooks in about fifteen minutes. Uncooked
it measures 4.57 mg per 100 g, but that is the dry weight — water is most of
what you serve, so the cooked number is the one that matters at the table.

**The iron is non-heme, and quinoa carries no vitamin C of its own** (USDA
records it at zero). CDC's advice for non-heme iron is to serve a vitamin-C food
in the same meal, so pair it: mashed [[broccoli]], [[mango]], or [[potato]]
alongside will do more for absorption than a larger helping of quinoa will.

It is not a replacement for [[fortified-infant-cereal]]. Fortified infant oat
cereal carries roughly forty times quinoa's cooked iron. Quinoa is a good
texture and a good habit; the fortified box is still the iron.

## Prep 6-8

Cook it with more water than the package says and longer than you would for
yourself, so the kernels are soft rather than springy. Then mash or grind them
and stir into something wet — puréed [[butternut-squash]], [[apple]], a
fortified cereal.

**The reason for the grinding step:** CDC lists whole cooked grain kernels as a
choking hazard and instructs you to cook and then finely grind or mash them.
Quinoa isn't named in that list, but "and other grains" is, and a cooked quinoa
kernel is a cooked whole grain kernel. **How finely** to grind is not something
any guideline body specifies, so use your own judgement: no distinct hard
kernels left, and it should smear rather than roll between two fingers.

Rinsing dry quinoa before cooking to wash off saponins is standard kitchen
practice and makes it taste less bitter. No guideline body issues it as a safety
step, and this record does not present it as one.

## Prep 9-11

Same cooking, less processing — mashed rather than ground, and stirred through
other foods so it does not scatter. Quinoa mixed into mashed [[lentils]] or
[[black-beans]] is a genuinely iron-dense bowl at this age, and the pincer grasp
makes small soft lumps something a baby can actually pick up.

## Prep 12-17

However the household eats it, cooked soft, in a bowl with other things rather
than dry and loose on a tray. Whole kernels are still kernels: keep them soft
and mash through anything that stayed firm.

## Safety

**Cooked whole grain kernels are a named choking hazard.** The modification is
CDC's: cook, then finely grind or mash. That instruction is issued; the degree
of grinding is not, so judge it by texture rather than by a rule.

If you serve quinoa in a mixed dish, AAP's one dimensional rule still applies to
everything else in the bowl — no piece larger than half an inch. That is the
only size rule any US guideline body issues, and everything else you read about
finger-lengths and thumbnails, this record included, is common practice.
