## Background

One of the few foods named by a guideline body for this exact moment: NHS lists
bananas among the fruits to offer from around six months, and AAP names small
pieces of banana as a suitable finger food. No cooking, no softening, no
equipment.

It is not an iron food. At 0.26 mg of non-heme iron per 100 g it is close to
the bottom of anything in this canon, so serve it for what it is — an easy,
familiar carbohydrate that a baby can practise on — and keep the iron work with
meat, fortified cereal, beans, and lentils.

Related: [[avocado]] is the other no-cook soft food and behaves much the same in
the hand.

## Prep 6-8

Peel about half the skin down, cut the banana in half across the middle, and
hand over the piece with skin still on the bottom half. Bare banana is
notoriously slippery; the skin is the grip.

If you want it bare, one common trick is to score the peeled banana lengthwise
into thirds so it separates into long spears that hold together better than a
whole one. That is a kitchen technique, not an instruction from anybody.

On size: AAP's published rule is to cut food for infants into pieces no larger
than one-half inch, and that is the only dimensional instruction a US guideline
body issues. Longer soft-food spears are widespread practice rather than
guideline advice, and they depend entirely on the fruit yielding under light
pressure.

## Prep 9-11

Once your baby picks things up between thumb and forefinger rather than raking
at them, small pieces stop being frustrating. Cut into short chunks, or leave
some longer pieces alongside so biting stays in practice.

## Prep 12-17

However the rest of the table is eating it — in chunks, sliced onto oatmeal,
mashed into yoghurt. No caveat beyond ripeness.

## Safety

Ripeness is the whole story. A ripe banana compresses to nothing; a firm,
green-shouldered one behaves like a hard raw fruit and should be left a few days
instead.

You may have been told never to serve banana in rounds. Worth knowing where that
comes from: CDC's rule about round foods is written about grapes, cherries,
berries, tomatoes, hot dogs, and string cheese — **banana is not on that list**,
and no guideline body prohibits banana coins. Ripe banana does not hold a rigid
circular shape the way a grape or a hot-dog slice does, which is the property
the rule is actually about. Cut it however suits your baby's grip.
