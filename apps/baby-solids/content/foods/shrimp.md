## Background

Shrimp is here for one reason: it closes the **crustacean shellfish** slot, one
of the nine major US allergens. It is not an iron food. At **0.51 mg of iron per
100 g** it contributes essentially nothing against a 7-to-12-month iron
requirement, and nothing in this record should be read as putting it in the same
job as [[beef]], [[sardines]] or [[salmon]]. What it does carry is protein —
**24 g per 100 g** — and zinc at 1.64 mg.

**The "shellfish" trap, stated plainly, because FDA and everyday language
disagree.** FDA's position is that crustacean shellfish is a major food allergen
and must be declared on a label, but **molluscan shellfish — oysters, clams,
mussels, scallops — is not**. Menus, relatives and most parenting advice treat
"shellfish" as one category. It is two. Shrimp, crab, lobster and crawfish close
the crustacean slot; no mollusc closes it. A mollusc allergy is real and can be
severe, it simply is not one of the nine and carries no mandatory declaration.

On mercury, FDA and EPA place shrimp in **Best Choices**, the lowest-mercury
group. But note what that advice does and does not say: it is written for
**children ages 1 to 11**, and its smallest serving size is one ounce for a one-
to three-year-old. **No US body publishes a seafood amount for a baby under
twelve months.** This record therefore gives you no weekly ounce figure, because
any figure in circulation has been extrapolated by somebody.

## Prep 6-8

Buy it peeled or peel it yourself: shell, tail and the dark vein all come off
**before** cooking, not after. A tail fin left on a cooked shrimp is a firm,
sharp-edged piece of shell in a baby's mouth.

Poach, steam or pan-cook until the flesh is opaque all the way through and firm
— NHS's rule is that shellfish is never served raw or lightly cooked to a baby,
and that is a food-safety rule, not a texture preference. Cooked shrimp is
springy and slippery, which is a bad combination at this age, so mince it fine
and stir it into something that holds it: mashed [[avocado]], [[sweet-potato]],
[[oatmeal]], a thick puree.

## Prep 9-11

Small pieces the pincer grasp can pick up, still cooked through, still with **no
piece larger than half an inch** — AAP's dimension is the only one any guideline
body issues, so it is the one this record uses. Shrimp firms up as it cooks; if a
piece feels rubbery to you, chop it smaller rather than trusting the gums.

## Prep 12-17

Cooked shrimp cut down to bite size, in whatever the household is eating — fried
rice, a noodle dish, a stew. Still fully cooked. Still no shell, no tail.

## Safety

**Never raw or lightly cooked.** That includes ceviche, "just pink" shrimp, and
anything from a raw bar. NHS names shellfish explicitly here.

**Shell and tail are the physical hazard, not the flesh.** Remove them before
cooking, when they are easy to see and pull.

**Shrimp is not on any official choking list by name.** The cutting instruction
in this record comes from applying CDC's general rules — cut cylindrical foods
into short thin strips, cook until it mashes easily — plus AAP's half-inch
maximum. That is category-level coverage, and this record says so rather than
implying a shrimp-specific ruling exists.

**Do not read a tolerated shrimp as a tolerated scallop.** Crustacean and
mollusc are separate allergens, and tolerating one says nothing about the other.
