## Background

Dense, sweet winter squash — 단호박 in Korean, where it's a common early
weaning food, often steamed and mashed thin. Behaves almost identically to
[[sweet-potato]] once cooked, so it's a natural second food if the first went
well: same shapes, same softness test, different flavour.

## Prep 6-8

Steam or roast until completely soft, then cut into finger-length wedges about
two fingers thick. Remove the skin for a beginner — kabocha skin stays chewy
even when the flesh is soft, and chewy is the harder texture.

## Prep 9-11

Soft cubes around thumbnail size once the pincer grasp arrives.

## Prep 12-17

Serve it the way the rest of the table eats it — cubed in a soup, mashed as a
side, or roasted in wedges. Skin can stay on now if it's roasted soft.

## Safety

The raw squash is very hard and needs real force to cut — that firmness is
exactly the hazard, so the softness test matters more here than with a
softer-fleshed vegetable. Press a cooked piece between thumb and forefinger
before serving; if it holds its shape under pressure, cook it longer.
