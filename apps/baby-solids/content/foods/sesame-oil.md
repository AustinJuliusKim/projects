## Background

Toasted sesame oil is in this canon as **flavour**, and only as flavour. A few
drops finish a bowl of rice, a plate of vegetables, a soup, a noodle dish — it
is one of the fastest ways to make plain infant food taste like the food the
rest of the family is eating, which matters more than it sounds like it should.

**It is not the way to introduce sesame, and this record carries no allergen
protocol for that reason.** The reasoning is worth stating rather than asserting:
allergen introduction depends on the baby eating enough of the **protein** to
matter, and oil is the fraction of the seed with the least protein in it.
Refined oils are generally low in allergenic protein. Unrefined and toasted oils
are variable — sometimes appreciable, sometimes not, and the label will not tell
you which jar you have. On top of that, **no guideline body anywhere sanctions
oil as an allergen-introduction vehicle.** There is no protocol, no dose, and no
statement that a baby who has had sesame oil has been introduced to sesame.

So: a baby who tolerates sesame oil has **not** had sesame introduced. Use
[[tahini]] or finely ground sesame seed for that job, and use the oil for
dinner. Treating the oil as the introduction is the failure mode this record
exists to prevent — it feels like the slot is closed when it is not.

The other half of that coin: a child with a **diagnosed sesame allergy** should
not be given sesame oil on the strength of "it's only oil." Sesame is a major
allergen and must be declared on US labels, and that declaration covers the oil.
The two statements are consistent — the oil is unreliable as a source of
allergen protein in both directions, which makes it useless for introduction and
unsafe for avoidance.

**No composition figures appear on this record.** A verified USDA entry for
toasted sesame oil could not be established while this was written, so there is
no id and no iron figure. It is a fat; treat it as one.

## Prep 6-8

A few drops, stirred through food that is already cooked and already cool enough
to eat. Toasted sesame oil has a low smoke point and is a **finishing** oil, not
a cooking one — heat it and you lose the aroma that was the entire reason for
adding it, and it turns bitter.

**How much is genuinely unverified.** No body publishes a quantity of oil for an
infant, so this record does not print one. A few drops over a portion is a
sensible reading of "flavour, not a food," and it is the honest limit of what
can be said.

Good places for it: a spoonful of rice porridge, mashed [[green-peas]], steamed
[[broccoli]] blitzed soft, plain [[tofu]].

## Prep 9-11

The same few drops, over more textured food. Sesame oil is what makes plain
steamed vegetables and mild proteins taste seasoned without salt, which is
useful at an age when the food is getting less interesting and salt is still off
the table.

## Prep 12-17

However the household uses it — over rice, into a dipping sauce, through
namul-style vegetables, on noodles. Still a finishing oil, still added off the
heat.

## Safety

**No choking risk.** Oil is not a hazard form, and sesame oil appears on no
hazard list. It is the seed and the seed butter that carry preparation rules —
see [[tahini]].

**The real risk with this food is a false sense of coverage.** If your reason
for serving sesame is to introduce the allergen, oil does not do it, and no
guideline says it does. Do the introduction with tahini or finely ground seed,
and log that instead.

**Check labels if sesame is being avoided.** Sesame has had to be declared as a
major allergen in the US since 1 January 2023, so a current label will name it.
An older jar, or an imported one, may not.
