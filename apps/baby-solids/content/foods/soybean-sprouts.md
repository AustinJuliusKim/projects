## Background

🚩 **콩나물 is soy. Soy is one of the nine major US allergens.**

That is the whole reason this food has its own page and its own medical gate,
and it is very easy to miss, because 콩나물 arrives at the table looking and
behaving like a vegetable — a side dish, a thing in the bottom of a soup bowl, a
crunchy handful in 비빔밥. Nothing about it announces "soybean."

**And it looks almost exactly like [[mung-bean-sprouts]] (숙주), which is not
soy and not a major allergen at all.** Side by side in a bowl the two are easy
to confuse. The tell is the head: 콩나물 has a distinct yellow soybean at the
top, noticeably larger and firmer. 숙주 has a small pale head and a shorter,
plumper stem. If you are tracking a soy introduction, or avoiding soy, that
difference is the thing to learn — see the 숙주 page for the same comparison
written from the other side.

If soy is what you are trying to introduce, [[tofu]] is a cleaner vehicle: more
soy protein per spoon, softer, and none of the string or cooking issues below.
콩나물 is worth knowing about mainly because it turns up in food the family is
already eating.

**Composition figures are not in this record.** The USDA database was
unavailable when it was researched, so there is no verified iron, zinc or
protein value and none is printed. The iron in a sprout is **non-heme** — a
classification, not a measurement. Non-heme iron absorbs better alongside a
vitamin-C food, so serving it with something like [[broccoli]] or
[[green-peas]] helps.

## Prep 6-8

🚨 **Cook it all the way through. Raw is out and so is lightly cooked.**

FDA's position on sprouts is unusually direct, and it is worth quoting the shape
of it. Sprouts are grown from seeds and beans in warm humid conditions — the
same conditions bacteria like — so a few organisms on the seed multiply to high
levels during sprouting, **even in a clean home setup**. FDA names *Salmonella*,
*Listeria* and *E. coli*. Its instruction is that people more vulnerable to
foodborne illness should avoid **"raw or lightly cooked sprouts of any kind"**,
and the list of vulnerable people opens with **children**.

Two things follow that families get wrong:

- **"Of any kind" covers 콩나물**, even though FDA's examples name onion,
  alfalfa, clover, radish and mung bean and never mention soybean. The rule is
  written to be general on purpose.
- **A blanch is not the mitigation.** "Lightly cooked" is prohibited alongside
  raw. The quick 30-second dunk that 콩나물무침 traditionally uses — the one that
  keeps the sprouts squeaky and crisp — is exactly the preparation FDA is ruling
  out. Cook them until they are properly soft.

Then handle the physical form:

1. **Trim the root tails.** The thin threadlike tail at the bottom is tough and
   stringy and does not soften with cooking. On 콩나물 many people also pull off
   the loose bean husks; that is a texture preference, not a safety step.
2. **Chop short — no longer than half an inch**, AAP's rule and the only
   dimensional rule any US guideline body issues. A cooked sprout stem is a long
   fibrous string, and length is the problem, not width.
3. **Chop fine and moisten** at this age — stirred through rice, [[oatmeal]] or
   a puree so it gathers rather than scattering.

Serve it unseasoned. 콩나물무침 and 콩나물국 are both salted; take the baby's
portion out before that.

## Prep 9-11

Same cooking, same tail trimming, same length limit. A pincer grasp picks up
chopped sprout pieces easily. Do not read improving hand skills as a reason to
serve longer pieces — the string is the hazard and a better grasp does nothing
about it.

## Prep 12-17

콩나물 as the household eats it, with two things unchanged: **thoroughly cooked**,
and **chopped short**. The raw-sprout rule does not expire at twelve months;
FDA's vulnerable list is about susceptibility, not about a birthday.

콩나물국밥 and 콩나물무침 are both realistic at this age with the salt reduced and
the sprouts cut down. Watch the 고춧가루 as much as the salt.

## Safety

🚩 **Soy — a major allergen wearing a vegetable's clothes.** This is the single
most important line on the page. See the medical gate above before the first
exposure, and watch for about two hours afterwards; IgE reactions occur within
minutes to two hours.

🚨 **Cooked thoroughly, always.** Raw and lightly cooked sprouts are both ruled
out for children by FDA. Sprouting conditions grow bacteria along with the
sprout, and washing does not fix it. A blanch does not count as cooking here.

**Strings.** Trim the tails, chop to under half an inch. Long fibrous stems are
the mechanical hazard.

**Do not confuse it with 숙주.** They look alike, one is a major allergen and
one is not.

**No guideline body addresses 콩나물 as an infant food.** FDA's sprout rule is
issued and covers it through "of any kind"; soy's allergen status and the
half-inch limit are issued. The tail trimming and the chop length applied to
this specific food are category rules plus ordinary practice.
