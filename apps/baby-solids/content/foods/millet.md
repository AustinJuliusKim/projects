## Background

Millet cooks quickly, is mild, and is a normal part of mixed-grain rice in
Korean households — 기장밥 and 조밥 are the everyday forms. It is worth having in
the rotation for variety and texture.

**It is not an iron food, and this is the thing to be clear about.** Cooked
millet carries **0.63 mg of iron per 100 g — the lowest of any grain in this
part of the canon.** Cooked [[quinoa]] is more than twice that, [[teff]] more
than three times, and [[fortified-infant-cereal]] is in a different category
entirely at around a hundred times.

That does not make millet a bad food. It makes it a food that should sit
*alongside* the iron rather than in place of it. The realistic failure mode is
quiet: a homemade multigrain porridge feels more wholesome than a box of
fortified cereal, replaces it, and the iron in the day drops by most of it
without anything visibly changing. If millet is doing a lot of the work in your
baby's bowl, put the iron somewhere else in the day — [[beef]], [[lentils]], or
fortified cereal.

Millet's iron, like every plant iron here, is non-heme, so a vitamin-C food in
the same meal helps: [[potato]], [[broccoli]], [[mango]].

## Prep 6-8

Cook with plenty of water until the grains have swollen and gone soft, then mash
or grind them and loosen with breast milk, formula, or the cooking liquid.
Millet gets creamy easily, which makes it a forgiving first porridge.

**Cooked millet grains are whole grain kernels**, and CDC's instruction for those
is to cook and then finely grind or mash. Millet is not named in that list, but
"and other grains" is. The grains are small enough that people skip this step —
the rule is written about kernels rather than about diameter, so do not.

## Prep 9-11

Softer-cooked millet mashed into other foods, or millet porridge with mashed
vegetables and a protein stirred through. Loose dry grains scatter and get
scooped by the handful; keeping it wet keeps it manageable.

## Prep 12-17

Mixed into the family's rice, in porridge, or in soups, kept soft. Millet in a
rice cooker with [[short-grain-rice]] is the normal household form and is fine
here, as long as the grains are soft and mashed through for anything still
distinctly firm.

## Safety

**Cooked grain kernels are on CDC's choking-hazard list** and the issued
instruction is to cook and then finely grind or mash them. Small size is not an
exemption in the way the rule is written.

The other risk with millet is nutritional rather than physical: **used as a main
grain, it displaces iron.** Treat it as texture and variety, not as a
contribution to the iron requirement.
