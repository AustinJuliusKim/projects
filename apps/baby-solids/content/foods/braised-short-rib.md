## Background

**The most useful thing to understand about 갈비찜 is that the beef and the dish
have different answers.**

The beef is a six-month food, and a good one. WHO's 2023 complementary feeding
guideline asks for animal-source foods daily from six months as a strong
recommendation, and beef carries **heme** iron — the form CDC says is absorbed
more readily than the non-heme iron in plants — plus zinc. Slow-braised short
rib is about as soft as beef gets. On its own merits it is close to an ideal
first protein.

**갈비찜 is a twelve-month dish, and the reason is 간장, not the beef.** The
sodium in the sauce is the constraint, and it is the only thing standing between
this dish and a much earlier start. Which means the practical move is available
to any family: **lift the baby's piece of meat out of the pot before the
seasoning goes in.** At that point it is braised beef, and braised beef is a
six-month food. The three prep sections below are built around that split.

🚩 **Two of the nine major US allergens arrive through the sauce, and the second
one surprises people.** Soy is obvious. **Wheat is not** — most brewed Korean and
Japanese soy sauces are made from soybeans *and* wheat, so 간장 typically carries
both. Families avoiding wheat routinely miss this. Read the bottle: some soy
sauces are wheat-free and say so, and the two are not interchangeable for a
child with a wheat allergy. We could not verify any figure for how much wheat or
residual gluten a given 간장 contains, so this record gives no threshold — only
the instruction to read the label.

If your reason for serving this dish is to get soy into the rotation, [[tofu]]
does that job better: it carries soy without the sodium and without the wheat
that rides along with 간장.

**Composition figures are not published here.** The USDA database was
unavailable when this record was researched, so there is no verified per-100-g
iron figure for short rib. We have deliberately **not** borrowed the ground-beef
number from [[beef]] — a different cut, a different fat content, a different
value. The iron *type* is heme; the amount is pending verification.

## Prep 6-8

**Braise the meat, then pull the baby's portion before you season.**

Practically, that means one of two things. Either braise a piece separately in
unsalted stock or plain water with the aromatics you like — garlic, onion, pear,
radish, all fine — or, if you are making one pot, take the baby's rib out early,
before the 간장 mixture is added, and finish it in a small pan with a little
unsalted liquid.

**Sodium is why, and it cannot be diluted away.** Sodium adequate intake is
110 mg a day from birth to six months and 370 mg from seven to twelve months
(NASEM 2019). There is **no upper limit and no EAR for sodium at any age** — so
370 mg is an adequacy target rather than a ceiling anyone issued. It is still
small enough that a single seasoned adult portion of 갈비찜 overwhelms it. Once
the sauce is in the braise it is in the meat, and adding water or rice does not
take it back out. Pulling the portion before seasoning is widely advised and
sound, but it traces to no numbered rule — treat it as practice.

**Then take it apart, in this order:**

1. **Meat off the bone**, after braising. 🚩 Note the conflict this creates and
   we are not going to hide it: **CDC says remove bones *before* cooking**, and
   갈비찜 by definition does not — the bone is what makes the dish. Taking the
   meat off afterwards is the closest available compliance, and it is our
   accommodation, not CDC's instruction. "Bones in meat or fish" is a named
   entry on CDC's choking-hazard list.
2. **Remove the fat and the cartilage.** Short rib has a lot of both, and
   cartilage stays rubbery no matter how long it braises.
3. **Shred along the grain**, not across it. Shredding along the grain gives
   soft separable strands; cutting across leaves short dense blocks that behave
   like the "tough or large chunks of meat" CDC lists as a hazard.
4. **Nothing over half an inch** — AAP's rule, and the only dimensional rule any
   US guideline body issues.
5. **Moisten** with a spoonful of the unsalted braising liquid so the shreds
   hold together instead of scattering.

🚨 **Never hand a baby the rib bone.** Not to gnaw on, not to hold, not to
"keep them busy while you eat." It is a hard bone with meat and cartilage
attached, and it is on the hazard list in its own right.

## Prep 9-11

No change in principle: still the unseasoned meat, still off the bone, still
shredded along the grain. A pincer grasp makes shreds much easier to pick up
than they were at seven months, so this is often when braised beef finally gets
eaten in quantity rather than pushed around.

You can serve slightly longer strands now — a two-inch shred that the baby bites
from is fine as long as it pulls apart easily under light pressure. If it holds
together when you tug at it, it needs more braising or a different piece.

## Prep 12-17

**This is where 갈비찜 itself becomes reasonable**, seasoned, as the family eats
it — and the reason is that the sodium context changes: adequate intake rises to
800 mg a day for a one-to-three-year-old.

Even so, it is a salty dish. Serve it as part of a meal with plenty of unsalted
rice and vegetables rather than as the whole plate, and do not make it the
everyday dinner. If you are cooking a pot for the week, seasoning it a little
lighter than usual costs the adults very little.

The physical rules do not relax: **meat off the bone, fat and cartilage
removed, shredded or cut small, no rib bone handed over.** "Tough or large
chunks of meat" stays on CDC's hazard list past the first birthday.

## Safety

**Two entries from CDC's choking-hazard list apply to this one dish** — "tough or
large chunks of meat" and "bones in meat or fish." That is unusual and it is why
this record is as specific as it is.

**The bone.** CDC's rule is to remove bones before cooking. 갈비찜 cannot comply,
because the bone is the dish. Take the meat off after braising, and never hand
the bone to the baby. That accommodation is ours.

**The meat.** Shred along the grain, remove fat and cartilage, keep pieces under
half an inch. Braised short rib is soft when it is properly done — if it does
not pull apart under light pressure, it is not ready, and cutting it smaller is
not a substitute for cooking it longer.

**Sodium is the reason this is a twelve-month dish, and it is not adjustable by
dilution.** Braise the baby's portion separately or in unsalted stock. This is
the single most important thing on this page for a family that wants to serve
갈비찜 early: the beef was never the problem.

**Two allergens, one of them hidden.** Soy and — through 간장 — wheat. See the
medical gate above. Read the soy sauce label; wheat-free 간장 exists but is not
the default.

**No guideline body addresses 갈비찜 as an infant food.** What is issued: WHO on
animal-source foods, CDC on bones and chunks of meat and on removing bones
before cooking, AAP's half-inch limit, FDA on soy and wheat as major allergens,
NASEM's sodium intakes. Everything specific to this dish — the pull-before-
seasoning move, shredding along the grain, taking the meat off the bone after
braising — is inherited category rule plus our inference.
